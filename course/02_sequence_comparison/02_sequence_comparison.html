Sequence comparison

Every comparative result is a conditional statement: if these sequences are homologous, if the alignment represents positional homology, if the model is adequate — then the tree follows. No software supplies those premises.

1
Before anything

Six different claims, routinely collapsed into one

This distinction comes first, because once BLAST is running it is too late. Readers silently convert an E-value into evolutionary and then functional annotation, and every step of that conversion is a separate claim requiring separate evidence.

ClaimWhat it assertsWhat actually supports it
similarityThese sequences score highly against each otherA score, under a stated matrix and gap cost. A measurement
alignabilityPositions can be put in correspondenceAn optimisation result — and it succeeds even on unrelated sequences
homologyCommon ancestral sequenceA historical inference. Binary. Never a percentage
domain membershipA region matches a family profileA profile hit — which may cover 15% of the gene
orthologySeparated by speciationA gene tree, or synteny. Never a best hit (Ch 05)
functionDoes the same jobAn assay. Nothing above implies it

The chain that breaks quietly

high score → alignable → homologous → same domain → ortholog → same function — six inferential steps, each defensible on its own, presented in most papers as one arrow.

The gate

Is this sequence even usable?

Primary-sequence errors can dominate downstream phylogenetic and selection results. Check before aligning, not after a surprising answer.

ORF integrity
Internal stops and frameshifts wreck codon alignments — and Chapter 11 runs on codon alignments
pseudogenes
A decayed copy in the set adds real substitutions that mean nothing adaptive
chimeras & fusions
One "gene" that is two; its two halves have different histories
annotation artefacts
Fragmentation and mispredicted boundaries — Chapter 04's failure modes arriving here
contamination
A foreign sequence aligns beautifully to its true relatives
domain architecture
Two proteins sharing one domain are not comparable end-to-end
3
Alignment

It optimises a score, not historical truth

Global alignment assumes full-length correspondence is a meaningful hypothesis; local alignment seeks one high-scoring region. Affine gaps (open + k×extend) encode a preference for one indel event over many short ones.

The consequence that matters downstream

Changing gap-open, gap-extension or the substitution matrix moves indel boundaries and forces different residues into columns — which changes what every downstream program counts as a substitution. The alignment is an input hypothesis, not an observation.

PAM

extrapolated

Built from replacements observed among close proteins, then extrapolated. One PAM = an average accepted change at 1% of positions.

BLOSUM

observed

Log-odds scores estimated directly from conserved ungapped blocks, clustering sequences above the matrix's identity threshold.

NCBI's practical defaults
  BLOSUM62      general purpose
  BLOSUM45      long, weak similarity        lower number → more distant
  PAM30 / PAM70 short queries

These are DETECTION HEURISTICS, not estimates of divergence time.
Choosing BLOSUM45 does not mean you believe the divergence is deep.
4
Search

What an E-value is, and what it is not

Karlin–Altschul, ungapped local scores:

    E ≈ K·m·n·e^(−λS)

    m,n = query and database lengths   →  E depends on the SEARCH SPACE
    S   = score                        →  E depends on the SCORING SYSTEM

So "significant" changes when the database grows. The same alignment,
the same biology, a different E-value next year.

An E-value is not a probability of homology

It is the expected number of chance local scores at least as good, under a specified random-sequence model. It is not a posterior probability of homology, and it is certainly not evidence of orthology or function.

A top hit means "best database match under this query, this database, and this scoring procedure". Domain sharing, uneven database representation, fast evolution, gene loss, fusion/fission and paralogy each invalidate the leap to "homolog".

Mask low-complexity and repetitive sequence, and inspect compositional bias. NCBI protein BLAST applies conditional compositional score adjustment by default; DIAMOND applies tantan repeat masking by default. Defaults are versioned methods metadata — record them.

5
Reach

Profiles — the answer when pairwise search runs out

Profile HMMs keep position-specific residue and indel distributions from an MSA, so they detect remote family relationships pairwise similarity misses. They fail differently, which is why they are not simply better.

Seed bias

failure

A profile inherits the errors and taxonomic skew of the alignment it was built from.

Domain ≠ gene

failure

A hit may represent one domain, not the full-gene history you are about to infer.

False transfer

failure

A weak partial domain match becomes a confident functional annotation downstream.

6
The big one

Multiple alignment is a source of error, not a preprocessing step

How large the disagreement actually is

On difficult empirical cases, one assessment found only 34–52% of columns correctly aligned across PRANK, MAFFT, ClustalW and T-Coffee. No aligner wins under every simulation, data type, indel process or downstream task.

And it manufactures selection

In the 12-Drosophila reanalysis, ordinary aligners placed non-homologous codons in the same column, producing false branch-site positives — and stripping gap columns did not remove them. Codon-aware PRANK outperformed PRANK-amino-acid, MAFFT, MUSCLE and ClustalW.

Markova-Raina & Petrov 2011, Genome Research · doi:10.1101/gr.115949.110

Codon-aware alignment is mandatory when the question is dN/dS. Protein-guided back-translation preserves reading frame and codon columns; MACSE additionally models frameshifts and stop codons, where PAL2NAL-style workflows cannot handle unexpected frameshifts automatically.

The filtering controversy — genuinely unresolved

Filtering helps

2007

Gblocks-style removal improved simulated protein-tree analyses.

Talavera & Castresana, Syst Biol

Filtering hurts

2015

Gblocks, trimAl, BMGE and ZORRO generally worsened single-gene phylogeny as more columns were removed — discarding signal along with noise.

Tan et al., Syst Biol

Targeted filtering helps

2019

HmmCleaner/PREQUAL-style segment filtering helps specifically against primary-sequence errors — a different problem from column noise.

Di Franco et al., BMC Evol Biol

The resolution is not a winner. It is that block filtering and error-segment filtering target different problems, and reporting which you used — with the alternative — is the minimum standard.

7
Models

Likelihood, selection, and the adequacy question nobody asks

A substitution model is a continuous-time Markov process. JC69 assumes equal base frequencies and equal rates; GTR allows unequal frequencies and reversible exchangeabilities; +G models site-rate heterogeneity and +I adds invariant sites. Felsenstein pruning computes the site likelihood by recursively summing over unobserved ancestral states.

What likelihood is

It is P(alignment | tree, model). It is not the probability that the inferred tree is true. Every downstream support value inherits that distinction.

Model SELECTION

routine

ModelFinder/jModelTest compare candidates by AIC/BIC. In many settings the criterion has little effect on topology; branch lengths, support values and hard regions are more sensitive — though under misspecification in difficult regions topology can change.

Model ADEQUACY

skipped

Asks whether the selected model could plausibly have generated the observed data at all — posterior-predictive or parametric-bootstrap thinking.

A selected model can still be inadequate. Selection picks the best of a bad set; adequacy asks whether the set was bad.

+G is not a general cure. Lineage-specific composition, heterotachy, recombination and saturation each violate assumptions that +G does not address — and each is waiting in Chapters 08 and 11.

8
The practice

Report what survives a sensitivity set

This is the chapter's operational deliverable, and it is what separates a defensible comparative result from a lucky one.

THE SENSITIVITY SET  — vary, then report only what is stable

  aligner         MAFFT · PRANK · MUSCLE      (codon-aware if dN/dS)
  filtering       none · block · error-segment
  model           selected · one simpler · one richer
  homolog set     with and without the marginal members
  taxon sampling  with and without the long branch

claim   survives all of the above    → report it
       survives some                → report it WITH the range
       survives one configuration   → you have a configuration, not a finding

Why this is the chapter's real content

Alignment uncertainty is empirically large, filtering is contested, and model adequacy is rarely checked. None of that is fixable by picking better defaults. It is fixable by reporting the range — which also happens to be the cheapest thing to demand as a referee.

You have understood Chapter 2 when…

Five things, without notes

  1. Separate similarity, alignability, homology, domain membership, orthology and function, and say what evidence each needs.
  2. Explain why an E-value changes when the database grows, and what it is not.
  3. State how much aligners disagree on hard cases, and why gap-stripping does not fix it.
  4. Give both sides of the filtering controversy without picking a winner.
  5. Distinguish model selection from model adequacy in one sentence each.
Self-check

A paper reports 62 genes under positive selection (branch-site, p < 0.05 after FDR) across 20 mammals. Methods: orthologs by reciprocal BLAST, aligned with MAFFT on amino acids then back-translated, Gblocks-filtered, PAML with the best model from ModelFinder. What is wrong before you look at a single gene?

homolog set
Reciprocal BLAST for orthology — Chapter 5's problem, and it puts paralogs into codon alignments.
alignment
MAFFT on amino acids then back-translated is not codon-aware in the sense that matters. The 12-Drosophila result is precisely this configuration producing false positives.
filtering
Gblocks removes columns, but the documented failure is non-homologous codons surviving gap-stripping. It also may have discarded signal.
model
ModelFinder selected; nothing tested adequacy. Composition shift across 20 mammals is exactly the unaddressed violation.
reporting
One configuration, one number. No sensitivity set, so there is no way to know whether 62 is stable or an artefact of these five choices.

None of this says the 62 genes are wrong. It says the paper has not yet given you grounds to believe them — which is the distinction this whole chapter exists to make.
