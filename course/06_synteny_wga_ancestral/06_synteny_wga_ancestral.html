Synteny, whole-genome alignment, ancestral genomes

A failed search is not an absence. Collinearity locates the place a gene should be, independently of the gene — which is the only way to tell loss from not-detected.

1
The answer, first

Why similarity cannot establish loss

A failed BLAST or orthology hit follows equally from gene-model failure, an assembly gap or collapse, high divergence, pseudogenisation, or paralogue reassignment. The search failing tells you about the search.

What collinearity adds

It locates the expected locus independently of the missing gene. If both flanking syntelogs map in conserved order and orientation to a single orthologous interval, you now have a place to point at — and absence from that interval is a different, much stronger claim than absence from a search.

2
The standard

What a gene-loss claim requires, in order

convergent evidence for loss  — each step is cheap, and each can kill the claim

1  conserved flanks      both syntelogs map, in order and orientation,
                          to ONE orthologous interval
2  interval is empty     no intact gene there, by direct DNA search —
                          not by annotation, which is what failed
3  search unannotated    protein and transcript search against the raw
                          sequence, not the gene set
4  assembly evidence     read depth and continuity EXCLUDE a gap or collapse
5  polarise              outgroups and close relatives place the event on a
                          branch — otherwise you have a difference, not a loss

Step 2 is the one that gets skipped

Searching the annotation for a gene that the annotation failed to call is circular. The search has to run against genomic sequence.

3
Aligners

Choose by what you have, not by divergence alone

There is no credible universal divergence cutoff

Repeat content, duplications, assembly continuity and rearrangement rate matter at least as much as ANI. "Use tool X above 90% identity" is not a rule anyone can support.

Why reference-free matters

Pairwise-to-one-reference alignment cannot represent non-reference-specific sequence, duplications, or rearranged loci. A reference-free MSA represents homology among all genomes and permits multiple orthology — which is exactly what a clade-scale comparison needs and a reference-based one silently discards.

HAL stores that hierarchical alignment including ancestral nodes; Cactus tooling exports MAF, chains, FASTA, conservation tracks and PhyloP, and supports liftover and comparative annotation. Record the exact version and configuration — releases through v3.2.1 (May 2026) changed graph-mapping, HAL compression, conservation and pangenome behaviour.

4
Synteny tools

Three jobs that get confused

MCScanX

detect

Anchor-based collinearity — the canonical toolkit for finding syntenic blocks from gene anchors.

GENESPACE

constrain

Constrains orthology with synteny, and explicitly models copy-number and tandem-array context — the direct answer to Chapter 05's differential-loss trap.

SynGAP

repair

Uses syntenic evidence to polish annotation (2024). It is not an aligner, and it is not a synteny detector — it fixes gene models.

For rearrangements specifically, pairwise WGA plus SyRI identifies syntenic regions first, then classifies inversions, translocations, duplications and local differences. At clade scale, map each event onto the species tree and validate key breakpoints independently.

Scaffolding manufactures rearrangements

Scaffold joins can mimic inversions, relocations and translocations. Hi-C has known orientation limitations at short distances, and reference-guided scaffolding can simply impose the reference arrangement on the new genome — producing "conserved synteny" that is an artefact of the method.

So a breakpoint claim needs independent support: another assembly, long reads spanning the junction, optical maps, or cytogenetics.

Ancestral genome reconstruction

This literature was entirely absent from the corpus — the ancestral_reconstruction directory was created empty on purpose. Here is what belongs in it.

Legitimately supports

use it for

Comparative coordinates · mapping rearrangements onto a tree · hypotheses about ancestral karyotype · conserved gene neighbourhoods.

Does not support

do not claim

Base-perfect ancient genomes · unqualified statements about repetitive DNA being absent or rearranged in the ancestor — repeats are exactly what these methods cannot reconstruct.

The framing to keep

Ancestral reconstruction is useful and is not time travel. Every output is conditional on a guide tree, an alignment, and in one case a reference. Treat a reconstructed ancestor as a hypothesis with a stated dependency, the way you would treat any other model output.

Acceptance test

You have understood Chapter 6 when…

Five things, without notes

  1. Give the five-step evidence standard for a gene-loss claim, in order.
  2. Say why a failed search is not an absence, and name four other causes.
  3. Explain what reference-free WGA represents that pairwise-to-reference cannot.
  4. Name three ways scaffolding manufactures a rearrangement.
  5. State what a reconstructed ancestral genome does and does not license.
Self-check

A paper reports that 47 immune genes were lost in a bat lineage, based on absence from the annotated gene sets of three bat genomes but presence in mouse and human, concluding immune-system streamlining associated with flight and viral tolerance. What do you require?

1 · the interval
Syntenic flanks for each of the 47 in each bat genome. Absence from a gene set is not absence from a genome.
2 · raw search
tblastn against genomic sequence, not the annotation that failed. Immune genes are fast-evolving — precisely the category annotation misses.
3 · gaps
Read depth across each interval. If any sits in or beside an assembly gap it is unusable.
4 · one event or 47?
Are they clustered? A single deleted array is one loss, not 47 — the same counting error as Chapter 7's tandem arrays.
5 · polarisation
Outgroups. Mouse and human are not an outgroup pair for bats; without a proper outgroup, "lost in bats" may be "gained in the mouse/human lineage".

And the framing check: fast-evolving immune genes are the single most likely category to produce false losses by all four mechanisms at once — divergence past detection, annotation failure, tandem-array collapse and lineage-specific turnover. A plausible story again arriving exactly where the artefacts are densest.
