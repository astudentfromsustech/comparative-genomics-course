The answer, first

One precondition holds up the whole field

The precondition

A genomic difference is interpretable only inside a homology relationship and an evolutionary time frame.

Without homology you don't know you compared the same thing. Without a time frame you don't know how old the difference is, which lineage it arose on, or how many independent chances it had to arise. Orthology inference, synteny, species trees, codon models, pangenome graphs — all of it exists to establish those two things.

The boring explanation is nearly always an artefact, and nearly always cheaper to test. That asymmetry — not its prior probability — is why it goes first.

3
Vocabulary

Four pairs that look like synonyms

Homology ≠ similarity

binary

Homology is a historical claim: common ancestral sequence. Never “40% homologous”. Similarity is a measurement, and only evidence for it.

Convergence produces similarity without homology; true homologs fall below any threshold you pick.
Fitch 1970

Ortholog ≠ paralog

event-defined

Both are homologs. Speciation separates orthologs, duplication separates paralogs. Orthologs are the only defensible “same gene” across species.

A paralog in a codon alignment manufactures dN/dS > 1.
ch 3 · ch 9

Conservation ≠ function

evidence types

Conserved means constrained — mutations were removed. It does not mean the function you care about, and fast-turnover enhancers are functional without being conserved.

ENCODE called ~80% of the genome biochemically functional; most of it is not constrained.
ENCODE 2012 · ch 12

Positive selection ≠ adaptation

four rungs apart

A branch-site test says a model with ω > 1 fits better. It says nothing about what the gene does or which environment favoured it.

Most papers cross that distance in one sentence.
ch 9 · ch 13
4
The anti-pattern

The template the field is leaving behind

BUSCO → OrthoFinder → tree → CAFE → PAML → GO enrichment → Circos
every box legitimate · every output read straight as a mechanistic conclusion

Analysis coverage is not evidence grade

Seven analyses are not stronger than one done well. When refereeing, the panel count in Figure 3 tells you nothing. Ask instead whether any analysis could have come out the other way.

The boundary rule

Comparative genomics establishes that a difference is real. It rarely establishes why. A paper claiming otherwise is usually skipping a neighbour.

Acceptance test

You have understood Chapter 1 when…

Four things, without notes

  1. Name the discipline that interprets a given result — without naming a tool.
  2. Say why homology ≠ similarity and positive selection ≠ adaptation, one sentence each.
  3. Take any row of the candidate table and list the boring explanations first, unprompted.
  4. Explain why seven analyses are not stronger than one done well.
Self-check

“We found 340 rapidly evolving genes enriched for immune function, showing immune adaptation in this lineage.” Name every layer at which this could be wrong, and the one check you would ask for first.

F1 · ask first
Were both genomes annotated the same way? Differential annotation alone manufactures the family-size differences and the alignment errors driving dN/dS.
F2
Paralog contamination in the alignments.
F3
Was the branch specified in advance, or chosen after looking?
F5
340 tests, correction unstated.
F6
GO enrichment against an inappropriate background.

“Enriched for immune function” is also the weakest available functional evidence: immune genes are the most numerous and fastest-evolving category in most genomes, so they surface in nearly every scan. Their appearance is uninformative, not confirmatory.
