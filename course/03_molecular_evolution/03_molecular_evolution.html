Molecular evolution

You need a generative model, not a catalogue of tests. The genome is a correlated set of local genealogies — so a dN/dS value, an FST peak or a discordant gene tree is a statistic several competing histories could produce.

1
Forward time, first

Frequencies before histories

The coalescent runs backward, which is elegant and unintuitive. One page forward-in-time makes it readable.

an allele's frequency changes each generation under four forces:

  mutation     introduces it                    rate μ
  drift        random sampling of 2N gametes    strength ∝ 1/Ne
  selection    changes expected contribution    coefficient s
  migration    imports frequencies              rate m

what decides which force wins is the SCALED parameter, not the raw one:

  Ne·s   ≪ 1  →  drift dominates; the allele is effectively neutral
  Ne·s   ≫ 1  →  selection dominates
  Ne·μ, Ne·m, Ne·r   likewise

A new neutral allele fixes with probability 1/2N and takes ~4Ne generations.
Almost all are lost. This is why "most substitutions" is a claim about
the mutations that survived, not about the mutations that occurred.

The one idea to carry forward

The same mutation can be effectively neutral in a small population and efficiently selected in a large one, because only the product Nes matters. Selection efficacy is therefore a property of the population, not of the mutation.

2
The null

Neutrality is a null, not a belief — and not one thing

"Neutral" is indispensable because it supplies explicit predictions for diversity, the site-frequency spectrum, linkage disequilibrium, fixation and gene-tree variation. It is not a claim that biology is neutral.

Neutral theory

Kimura 1968

Most molecular substitutions are neutral. Its classic result: the neutral substitution rate equals the neutral mutation rate — independent of population size.

Nearly-neutral theory

Ohta 1973

Adds weakly selected mutations whose fate depends on Nes. So lineages with different Ne differ in both polymorphism and protein evolution rate.

The distinction almost everyone skips

Neither the original nor the nearly-neutral theory is the strict-neutrality null used in tests. They are substantive hypotheses about biology; the null is a model. Treating "neutral theory" and "the null" as the same object is what makes the neutralist/selectionist dispute look settled when it is not.

Where the field actually stands

Not "selection versus neutrality". Direct selection, weak selection, drift and linked selection all contribute, with relative importance varying by taxon, genomic region and question. Claims about the proportion of adaptive substitutions remain model-dependent, because demography, the distribution of fitness effects, gBGC and linkage all confound them.

The discipline

Identifiability — different histories, same signature

This belongs immediately after neutrality, because it is what stops a null from becoming a rubber stamp. Neutral expectations are conditional on the mutation model, sampling, structure, recombination and ascertainment.

4
Forces

Mutation, recombination, gene conversion, linked selection

Mutation is not a clock

2× revision

Directly counting de novo mutations in pedigrees gave human rates roughly twofold below older indirect evolutionary estimates — materially changing divergence-time interpretations, and raising mutation-rate evolution as a live possibility.

Ignored, rate and spectrum heterogeneity reads as lineage-specific selection or accelerated evolution.

Recombination

structural

Breaks associations and lets local genealogies differ along a chromosome. Its rate is heterogeneous.

Ignoring that heterogeneity makes sweep signals and demographic inferences look more spatially coherent than they are.

gBGC

mimics selection

GC-biased gene conversion preferentially transmits GC over AT alleles, and can drive even deleterious GC alleles to fixation — including at coding sites.

A non-selective process that produces the signature of positive selection. High downstream cost in Ch 11 and 13.

Linked selection

pervasive

Background selection (purifying selection on linked deleterious alleles) reduces linked neutral diversity and alters coalescence times; sweeps do the same by hitchhiking.

In one human analysis, BGS and gBGC jointly affected up to 95% of variants and biased demographic inference even at synonymous and non-transcribed sites. Human-specific — not a universal figure
5
After the forces, not before

Ne is not a headcount

This section comes after linked selection deliberately. Meet Ne too early and you reify it as a single number for a species.

What it actually is

The size of an idealised population that matches a specified process — coalescence, drift, inbreeding, or variance in allele-frequency change. These give different answers. There is no single Ne.

varies in time
Bottlenecks and expansions; a PSMC curve is a trajectory, not a constant
varies along the genome
Linked selection makes local Ne lower where recombination is low
varies by estimator
Which process you measured determines which number you get
is not census size
Skewed reproductive success, sex ratio, age structure and migration all separate Ne from N

Treating Ne as census size miscalibrates drift, every mutation-scaled parameter, selection efficacy, and divergence dates — which is to say, most of Chapters 11 and 12.

6
Backward time

The coalescent, and what breaks it

Drift, viewed backward from a sample: follow lineages until they merge, then place mutations on the resulting random genealogy. Polymorphism is a consequence of tree shape.

What the elementary coalescent assumes

A panmictic, randomly mating Wright–Fisher-like population of constant size; neutral, non-recombining loci; simple mutation. Population structure, selection and recombination each require a different extension — and each is the norm, not the exception, in comparative datasets.

The multispecies coalescent embeds within-population coalescence inside species-tree branches, predicting gene-tree discordance through incomplete lineage sorting on short internal branches. Discordance is therefore expected under no gene flow — the single most important thing Chapter 08 inherits from here.

7
Before the SFS

From reads to genotypes — where the spectrum is decided

The site-frequency spectrum counts derived alleles by sample frequency, and it is the workhorse summary of Chapters 11 and 12. What it looks like is decided upstream, by data generation — which is why this section precedes it.

Hard genotype calls throw away uncertainty, and it biases the SFS

In simulation, call-based allele counting biased the spectrum in opposite directions depending on the calling strategy: multisample calling can understate rare variants, single-sample calling can overstate them. Both then bias demographic and selection inference.

Genotype-likelihood methods (ANGSD-style) integrate base and mapping-quality evidence instead of pretending an uncertain genotype was observed. Required whenever depth or error uncertainty is material relative to the signal: ancient DNA, low-pass resequencing, pooled or uneven data, and anything dominated by rare variants.

reference bias
Reads matching the reference map better — a systematic, directional error
paralogs & SVs
Mismapping there creates false heterozygotes and false variants
ancestral state
The SFS needs polarisation; outgroup error folds into the spectrum
ascertainment
How sites were chosen determines what frequencies you can even see
missingness
Non-random missing data is non-random frequency data

So an SFS departure is evidence against a specified neutral-demographic model. It is not a selection call, and demography and selection are frequently non-identifiable from the spectrum alone.

Where it is going

From summaries to genealogies

An ancestral recombination graph is the correlated sequence of local genealogies along a genome — conceptually the right object for joint inference of demography, selection and ancestry.

Read the scale column carefully

ARG-Needle reaches biobank scale; SINGER's contribution is uncertainty, at hundreds of genomes. These are different achievements and are sometimes cited as though interchangeable. Estimated ARGs remain model-based and uncertain — the field is moving toward ARG-aware analysis, not abandoning summary statistics, which remain essential for robustness, speed and model checking.

9
The bridge

Divergence and polymorphism are the same process

This is the hinge between population genetics and everything the rest of this course does.

one mutational process, two parts of one genealogy

  polymorphism   mutation on recent, still-uncoalesced within-species branches
  divergence     mutation on branches separating lineages after shared ancestry

The boundary is NOT a biological wall:
  · ancestral polymorphism persists beyond speciation  → ILS (Ch 08)
  · gene flow crosses it                               → introgression (Ch 12)

consequence  substitution-only dN/dS and polymorphism-plus-divergence tests
             (McDonald–Kreitman) answer DIFFERENT questions. Neither is
             a better version of the other.

Why the course is ordered this way

Every later chapter is an application of this one section. Chapter 08 needs ancestral polymorphism to explain ILS. Chapter 11 needs the substitution process to have a null. Chapter 12 needs gene flow crossing the boundary. Chapter 13 needs all three at once.

Self-check

A paper reports a 400 kb region in a montane bird population with reduced diversity, elevated FST against the lowland population, and an excess of rare variants — concluding a selective sweep on an altitude-adaptation locus. What must be excluded first?

recombination
Is local recombination rate low there? Low-recombination regions show reduced diversity and elevated FST without any sweep.
background selection
Purifying selection on linked sites produces the same three signatures. It is the default alternative, not an exotic one.
gBGC
Check the base composition of the elevated substitutions. GC-biased gene conversion mimics selection and needs no adaptation.
data generation
What coverage, and were genotypes called or likelihood-based? An excess of rare variants is exactly what single-sample calling manufactures.
demography
A montane population is a strong prior for a bottleneck, which also produces an SFS distortion — genome-wide, so compare against the genomic background.
mutation rate
Reduced diversity can be reduced mutation, not increased removal.

Note that the three reported signatures are not three independent pieces of evidence — reduced diversity, elevated FST and SFS distortion are largely the same observation viewed three ways, and every alternative above produces all three together.
