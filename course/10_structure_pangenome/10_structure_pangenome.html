The answer, first

The question itself changes

from   "what differs at locus x?"

to     which paths are homologous?
       which sequence does each lineage traverse?
       is this apparent loss a deletion, an unassembled repeat, or an annotation mismatch?

That last distinction is decisive for presence/absence variation, copy number and gene-family evolution — which is to say, for Chapters 06 and 07.

Why VCF-against-GRCh38 is no longer sufficient as the sole language

The HPRC graph contains 129.3 Mb of non-GRCh38 sequence, and a reference-tree formulation identified 3.5 million variants not located on the linear reference. Sequence that is not in the reference has no coordinate to be reported at.

The cost, stated plainly

Equivalence becomes conditional on graph construction, path set, repeat resolution and coordinate convention. Graph topology and path sampling can change the alleles and breakpoints reported. So a claim must state the graph build, version and parameters, the input assemblies and their order, coordinate projection, repeat handling, and benchmarks stratified by difficult region — not only a merged VCF.

A nomenclature trap

The official HPRC site dates Data Release 2 to 12 May 2025 (>230 samples, phased near-T2T assemblies, annotations, graph alignments, methylation). The 460-haplotype description is a separate July 2026 preprint. These get conflated; they are different artifacts.

T2T

238 Mb that used to be a gap

CHM13 is 3.055 Gb and closes all autosomes and X telomere-to-telomere. It adds or corrects 238 Mb of sequence non-collinear to GRCh38.

what that 238 Mb is

  76 %   centromeric satellite
  19 %   non-satellite segmental duplication
   4 %   rDNA

These regions can now be framed as COMPARATIVE SEQUENCE rather than gaps:
centromere architecture, acrocentric/rDNA organisation, repeat expansions,
paralogous loci, SD-mediated rearrangement.

But CHM13 is one haplotype

It is not a population reference. Completeness and representativeness are different properties, and a complete single genome does not solve reference bias — it relocates it.

The graph you build determines the variants you find

What comparisons actually find

PGGB and Minigraph-Cactus retain substantially more small variation than minigraph. Published comparisons find broadly similar SV sets but different breakpoint and path precision, and different graph complexity. The disagreement is in representation, not mostly in discovery.

5
Calling and comparing

Merging SVs is an equivalence problem, not bookkeeping

Discovery

no winner

Long-read alignment callers — Sniffles2, cuteSV, SVIM, pbsv, SVDSS — and assembly-comparison approaches like dipcall and SVIM-asm.

Caller, aligner, technology, coverage, SV class and truth-set region all change the result. No caller is uniformly best.

Genotyping

scales

vg/Giraffe maps reads to haplotype-threaded graph paths; PanGenie is k-mer-based for graph-represented variants.

Giraffe genotyped 167,000 known SVs in 5,202 short-read genomes.

Merging

the hard part

Jasmine jointly refines and merges population SV calls; Truvari benchmarks and collapses using sequence and breakpoint similarity.

Truvari's standard bench comparison is 1-to-1. Report thresholds, and whether alleles were sequence-harmonised before comparison.

Why merging deserves its own paragraph

Two callers reporting "the same" 8 kb deletion at slightly different breakpoints, with different inserted sequence, are making different claims. Whether they merge is a threshold decision that changes every downstream count — population frequency, PAV, gene-family size. It is not a formatting step.

The standard

What a PAV call must show

before "absent" means absent

1  high-quality assembly with INTACT FLANKS        Ch 6's syntenic interval
2  read remapping showing absence by BREADTH and DEPTH
3  repeat- and paralogue-aware sequence search
4  uniform reannotation or annotation lift-over    not two pipelines compared

One published Brassica rule: a gene is absent only when ALL exon bases have
< 2 reads AND < 5% exon breadth. That is an EXAMPLE threshold, not a
universal standard — but a paper with no such rule has no standard at all.

Expression, honestly

The strongest direct evidence is for allele- and haplotype-specific transcript quantification: mpmap+rpvg infers transcript-path abundance from a spliced graph and reduces ASE bias. I could not find a comparably established general improvement figure for human bulk RNA-seq across cohorts — so treat "graphs fix expression quantification" as unsupported in the general case.

Self-check

A crop paper builds a pangenome from 32 accessions with minigraph, reports 1,842 genes showing presence/absence variation, finds PAV genes enriched for disease resistance, and concludes that the dispensable genome is a reservoir of adaptive variation. What do you ask?

construction
minigraph is SV-oriented and approximate — it omits much small variation and gives imprecise breakpoints. For a gene-level PAV claim, that is the wrong instrument, or at least one needing a base-level cross-check.
backbone order
minigraph builds incrementally onto a backbone. Which accession was first, and does the PAV set change if the order changes?
absence evidence
Was absence established by read breadth and depth, or by a gene model failing to appear? The four-step standard, or none.
annotation uniformity
32 accessions annotated how? Non-uniform annotation manufactures PAV directly — the 15-fold result from Chapter 4.
the enrichment
Resistance genes are typically clustered, repetitive, tandemly arrayed and fast-evolving — the single category most prone to assembly collapse and annotation failure. Enrichment for them is what an artefact looks like as well as what biology looks like.

The honest reading is that the conclusion may be right — dispensable genomes really are enriched for resistance genes — while the evidence as presented cannot distinguish it from the artefact that mimics it.
