Orthology

Orthology is a claim about history, not similarity. The event that separated two genes is not observable — everything difficult follows from that.

1
The answer, first

The event fixes the relationship

Speciation gives orthology; duplication gives paralogy; horizontal transfer complicates both. Orthology is therefore not “same function”, not “most similar sequence”, not “one gene per species”. Each of those is a heuristic adopted because the event history is unavailable, and each fails in its own way.

There is no universal winner

The apparent best method changes with benchmark type, precision-versus-recall preference, submitted configuration, and whether a developer chose to publish their result. Anyone telling you method X is simply best is over-reading a benchmark.

An orthogroup is not a set of pairwise orthologs

It is a clade descending from one gene in the last common ancestor of the specified species set — defined relative to that set. It deliberately contains within-species paralogs, and it is not a taxon-independent “gene family”: change the species set and the groups change.

Chapter 5 feeds orthogroup sizes to a birth–death model and Chapter 9 feeds orthogroup members to codon models. Neither re-examines the boundary you handed it.

The core failure

Why reciprocal best hit misses so much

Sequence closeness proxies event history imperfectly. RBH holds up in simple prokaryotic settings, but in duplication-rich animals and plants it misses up to 60% of orthologous relations.

Benchmarks

Know their construction, not their rankings

Rankings move; construction problems do not.

Quest for Orthologs

developer submissions

Twelve tests — species-tree discordance, reference gene trees, functional concordance, curated mammalian relations — evaluated against proxies, since true histories are unknowable.

The aggregate rank depends on the classifier scheme; square-quartile privileges precision and reorders methods. “Top of QfO” is an incomplete sentence.
orthology.benchmarkservice.org

OrthoBench, revised

authored by a competitor

The original had 39% of RefOGs judged incorrect on reanalysis. The revision publishes inputs, trees, rationale and scoring code — but is 70 deliberately difficult Bilaterian groups, not genome-wide truth.

Best method: ~77% accurate, exactly recovering 23 of 70 groups. The revision was authored by OrthoFinder's developers.
Emms & Kelly 2020

What could not be verified

No independent 2025–2026 leaderboard compares OrthoFinder v3, current OMA StandAlone, SonicParanoid2, Broccoli and Proteinortho6 under one frozen dataset. SonicParanoid2's aggregate claim used QfO results from February 2023, with Broccoli absent. Treat any stronger “current winner” statement as unsupported.

Upstream

Your annotation is part of the inference

Different annotation sources yield materially different orthology calls. Short or fragmented proteins split one gene across groups or prevent matching; isoform choice changes comparisons, trees, and benchmark recall.

one protein per locus
Documented, consistent policy. Longest isoform is common and required by many tools; OMA can pick an evolutionarily conserved one instead
audit outliers
Length and domain outliers, before believing any group containing them
drop redundancy
Contamination and near-identical proteomes
keep coordinates
You need synteny to adjudicate the §3 case
reannotate first
Suspicious families, before interpretation — not after
7
The frontier

Do protein language models change this?

They improve the input, not the assignment

PLMSearch and embedding-to-Foldseek methods measurably improve remote-homology retrieval below ~20% identity — real, because that is where BLAST-family search goes blind. But no independent QfO or OrthoBench evidence shows they supplant gene-tree reconciliation for orthology assignment.

Detecting that two sequences are homologous and determining which event separated them remain different problems — Chapter 1's distinction.

Self-check

A manuscript builds single-copy ortholog sets across 12 plant genomes by reciprocal best BLAST hit, finds 4,102, concatenates them into a tree, and runs branch-site tests on all of them. Name the three compounding problems.

1 · worst case for RBH
Plants are duplication-rich with repeated WGD — exactly where RBH misses up to 60% of relations and differential loss converts out-paralogs into apparent orthologs.
2 · “single-copy” is a filter
Requiring one copy per species preferentially keeps genes that resisted duplication and silently admits families where all but one copy was lost. The set is both biased and contaminated.
3 · correlated downstream
The same mis-assigned genes enter the tree and every branch-site test, so the tree cannot check the alignments — and 4,102 tests with no stated correction compounds it.

The fix is not a better cutoff. It is gene trees plus syntenic verification, and reporting how many families were excluded and why.
