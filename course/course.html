What this course assumes

An earlier version of this page claimed Chapter 1 assumes no genomics. That was wrong.

A survey of eleven real curricula — MIT 6.047, Stanford CS262, Bologna 91407, Evomics, EMBL-EBI and others — found the course jumped straight to orthology and selection while assuming the reader could already judge an alignment, a substitution model and a likelihood calculation. Chapters 02 and 03 are that missing floor, added in response.

assumed
Programming, statistics, and comfort with bioinformatics tooling — you have run MAFFT and IQ-TREE
taught here
What those tools assume, and what their assumptions license you to claim
not taught
Unix, workflow managers, and lab methods. The Evomics workshop and Bologna 91407 cover these well

The frame

One precondition, six layers, one ladder

The precondition

A genomic difference is interpretable only inside a homology relationship and an evolutionary time frame. Everything else is machinery for establishing those two.

Three honest gaps

1. 79 of 172 PDFs are still missing, unevenly. They are paywalled — the open-access ladder cannot reach them, so chapters 11 and 14 cannot yet be written from the vault.

2. Two directories are empty on purpose. The field has those literatures; this corpus does not. Chapter 6 lists what belongs in the first of them.

3. The corpus inherits one article's citation list — a real bibliography, not a systematic survey. The research found what it omitted: OrthoFinder v3, the annotation-mixing experiment, FCS-GX, the bidirectional-best-hit critique.

If you forget everything else

The one paragraph

A difference between genomes means nothing until you know it is a real difference between corresponding things, and you know when and on which lineage it arose. Establishing that takes six nested layers of machinery, and a failure in any lower layer emerges from the top not as an error but as a plausible, publishable evolutionary story. So the discipline is not the pipeline — it is the ordered exclusion of the boring explanations, cheapest first, before the interesting one is entertained. Every method has a characteristic way of failing silently, and the mark of someone who knows the field is that they can name the failure before they name the tool.
