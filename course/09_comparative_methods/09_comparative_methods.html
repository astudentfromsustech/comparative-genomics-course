Phylogenetic comparative methods

One ancestral change plus many descendants can produce a very small P-value. This is the machinery that licenses every cross-species claim in Part VI — and the most common referee-level failure in the field.

1
The answer, first

Phylogenetic pseudoreplication

Each tip inherits most of its phenotype and genome from shared ancestors, so close relatives contribute correlated observations, not new evolutionary trials. Ordinary regression treats clade membership as repeated evidence.

2
A real casualty

What the correction actually costs a result

Rubisco kinetic trade-offs

Long-cited trade-offs were greatly reduced under phylogenetic reanalysis. The CO₂-specificity / carboxylation-turnover relationship fell from 37.4% to 2.2% explained variance — while phylogeny explained 56.1%.

The relationship did not vanish because the biology was fake. It shrank because most of the apparent signal was shared ancestry being counted as independent evidence.

And a failure mode specific to comparative functional genomics

Pairwise cross-species comparisons are non-independent when duplicated or ancestral comparisons are involved — and node-age and clade structure can manufacture apparent ortholog–paralog differences under null simulations. The difference appears with no biological cause at all.

The machinery

Three corrections, one shared assumption

MethodWhat it doesNote
PICConverts tip values to standardised, non-overlapping ancestral contrasts under Brownian motionThe original idea (Felsenstein 1985)

Phylogenetic
mixed models		Random phylogenetic effects; partitions phylogenetic vs residual variance; extends to multivariate and categoricalRetains assumptions about the phylogenetic covariance and exchangeable residual structure

None of them "corrects for phylogeny"

Each corrects residual covariance conditional on a supplied phylogeny and evolutionary covariance model. That means the correction inherits: the topology, the branch lengths, the trait model, measurement error, and — for genomic traits especially — whether the tree you supplied is the relevant history for that locus at all.

It does not establish causality, and it does not repair an incorrect tree.

4
Trait models

OU support is not evidence of stabilizing selection

BM

baseline

Constant-rate diffusion — a random walk. Variance accumulates with time.

OU

over-read

Mean reversion toward one or more optima. Routinely reported as evidence of stabilizing selection. It is not.

EB

rarely fit

Exponentially declining rate. Rarely supported in a broad trait survey — which does not prove adaptive radiations are rare, because extant-tip EB inference can lack power.

Why multi-regime OU fitting goes wrong

Unknown shift placement makes candidate models proliferate — a large-p, small-n model-selection problem. ML parameters can be non-unique or inaccurately estimated, and conventional criteria can favour excessive complexity. An AIC-only OU claim on a small or uninformative tree needs simulation-based calibration and model adequacy, and ideally fossils or independent experimental support.

The nuance that keeps this honest

"Small tree means OU is invalid" is too strong. Simulations found even relatively small trees can sometimes discriminate simple OU models — while parameter estimates remain poor. Discriminating a model and estimating its parameters are different achievements.

5
PhyloG2P

Genome-scale scans are association screens

MethodTestsAssumes / watch
RERconvergeBranchwise relative evolutionary rates, normalised genome-wide, associated with binary/continuous/categorical phenotype historiesThe basic workflow expects input gene trees to share topology
PhyloAccBayesian branch-category model for acceleration of conserved non-coding elements: null vs target-lineage vs unrestricted rate shiftTargets rate-pattern evidence, not a causal mechanism
Forward GenomicsRepeated loss / divergence associated with trait lossThe original required an all-or-none perfect match and had no phylogenetic or rate correction; the 2016 methods added both plus formal significance testing

Their nulls fail in predictable ways

Rate heterogeneity, incorrect foreground histories, gene-tree discordance, and correlated genomic features. Raw parametric P-values from phenotype-rate scans can be non-uniform because of phylogenetic and non-genetic dependence.

Permulations — permutations that preserve both the phenotype distribution and the phylogenetic structure — calibrate the null properly. They still require multiple-testing correction on top.

Do not quote a tool-level false-positive rate

There is no portable false-positive rate for RERconverge, PhyloAcc or Forward Genomics. Published benchmarks are simulation- and design-dependent. A paper should show empirical null calibration for its own tree and foreground configuration.

6
The standard

What a cross-species association must report

reportable record for a phyloG2P claim

  transition counts + uncertainty     how many INDEPENDENT origins, and how sure
  phylogenetic effective sample size  parameter- AND model-specific — not one number
  calibrated null / permulations      for THIS tree and THIS foreground
  genome-wide FDR                     after the calibrated null, not instead of it
  alternative-tree sensitivity        does the result survive a different topology?
  functional validation               independent of the comparative signal

How many independent origins are enough?

There is no defensible universal minimum — not three, not five, not ten. Treat any such rule as unsupported unless tied to a pre-specified power analysis.

What can be said: one origin supports a lineage-specific hypothesis, not a general cross-species genotype–phenotype claim. Power depends on tree geometry, effect size, the background lineages, and error — so the honest move is to report the effective sample size rather than the species count.

You have understood Chapter 9 when…

Five things, without notes

  1. Explain phylogenetic pseudoreplication, and why genomics makes it worse.
  2. Say what PGLS assumes and what it does not fix.
  3. State why OU support is not evidence of stabilizing selection — and why "small tree means OU is invalid" is also wrong.
  4. Explain what a permulation preserves that an ordinary permutation does not.
  5. Say why there is no universal minimum number of independent origins, and what to report instead.
Self-check

A paper reports that across 62 mammal species, genome size correlates with hibernation (PGLS, P = 0.003), that RERconverge finds 340 genes with hibernation-associated rate acceleration at FDR < 0.05, and concludes a genomic signature of hibernation. What do you ask?

origins
How many independent origins of hibernation are in those 62 species, with uncertainty on the ancestral reconstruction? If hibernation is largely one or two clades, effective n is 1–2 regardless of the 62.
PGLS is not enough
It corrects residual covariance given the tree and a BM-like model. It does not make two clades into 62 trials, and the Rubisco case shows how much apparent signal that can be.
null calibration
Were permulations run, for this tree and this foreground? Raw RERconverge P-values are non-uniform, so an FDR applied to them is an FDR on a wrong null.
topology assumption
RERconverge's basic workflow expects gene trees to share topology. With 62 mammals and real ILS (Ch 8), how was discordance handled?
the other trait
Hibernation covaries with body size, latitude, diet and metabolic rate. A correlation with genome size may be a correlation with any of them.
validation
340 genes is a screen output. What independent functional evidence exists for even one?

Note the two results are not independent confirmations of each other — both are computed on the same tree, from the same phenotype coding, and both inherit the same origin count.
