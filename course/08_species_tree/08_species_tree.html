The species tree

The tree is the coordinate system every comparison is expressed in. It is also a model of population splits — not a claim that any particular locus followed it.

1
The answer, first

What the tree is for

It assigns every sampled genome to a lineage, orders speciation events, roots and polarises substitutions, and defines the ancestral branch on which any event is mapped — a gene gain or loss, a rearrangement, a rate shift, a trait transition.

Which is why every later chapter takes it as input

Chapter 07's birth–death model needs it ultrametric and time-calibrated. Chapter 11's branch-site tests need the branch specified. Chapter 09's contrasts need its topology and lengths. Chapter 06's Cactus alignment needs it as a guide tree. A wrong tree is not one wrong figure — it is a wrong coordinate system.

2
The central fact

Gene trees are expected to differ

Under the multispecies coalescent, alleles can fail to coalesce within a short ancestral interval, so gene trees vary around the species tree. Discordance is a prediction of the null, not evidence of anything by itself.

3
The dispute

Concatenation versus coalescent — the honest version

Concatenation can be statistically inconsistent

Under MSC gene-tree heterogeneity, including anomaly-zone parameters, concatenated ML plus bootstrap can converge on a strongly supported wrong tree. More sequence yields more confidence in the pseudo-true supermatrix tree. This is a formal inferential failure, not a finite-data nuisance.

ASTRAL

scales

Estimates an MSC species tree from unrooted gene trees; statistically consistent under its model. Its advantage rises with moderate-to-high ILS — while concatenation performed better in the authors' own low-ILS simulations.

Inherits gene-tree estimation error, and is not robust to introgression, duplication/loss or linked loci merely because it is coalescent-based.

Full-likelihood MSC

scales poorly

*BEAST/StarBEAST2 and BPP jointly model sequence and gene-tree uncertainty rather than taking estimated gene trees as data.

Usually computationally restricted relative to ASTRAL-scale datasets — the trade is uncertainty handling against locus count.

The consensus is neither slogan

Not "concatenation is wrong", not "ASTRAL is automatically right". Current strong practice is plural: infer a partitioned concatenation tree and an MSC summary tree, quantify locus/site/quartet conflict, test robustness to locus definition and filtering, then investigate the biological causes of conflict that survives.

4
Support

Bootstrap does not mean what people report it to mean

MeasureActually measuresRead it as
bootstrapWhether resampled characters recover the fitted analysisResampling stability — not the fraction of genes or sites supporting the split
gCFProportion of decisive gene trees containing the splitEmpirical gene-level concordance
sCFProportion of decisive sites supporting itEmpirical site-level concordance. Use likelihood-based --scfl (IQ-TREE ≥ 2.2.2) over the original parsimony version
quartet freq / local PPASTRAL's support for the quartet resolutionCoalescent-model support

100% bootstrap with low gCF is routine

At genome scale and under model misspecification it is the normal outcome, not a red flag you rarely meet. Reporting bootstrap alone hides exactly the conflict the data contain.

Report instead: gCF, likelihood-based sCF, ASTRAL quartet frequencies and local posterior probabilities, branch lengths in coalescent units, and the sensitivity analyses — not one support number.

Filtering has no universal rule

Gene-tree error and missing data affect concatenation and MSC summary alike, and filtering can help or hurt depending on ILS and locus signal — so a single occupancy threshold is not defensible. Saturation-based locus exclusion improved inference across 36 empirical phylogenomic datasets, which supports the approach but does not validate filtering for your dataset. This is Chapter 02's sensitivity set again.

5
Dates

Sequence gives substitutions; time comes from priors

Sequence data primarily identify substitutions. Absolute time depends materially on rate assumptions and fossil/time priors — so a divergence date is a statement about the calibration as much as about the data.

Calibrations interact with the tree prior

The effective marginal prior for a calibrated node can differ when calibrations are used alone versus together. A consequence people miss: calibration "cross-validation" can therefore select bad constraints — the procedure that looks like a check is not one.

what a published date must expose

  calibration justification      why this fossil, this node, this bound
  effective marginal priors      what the model believed BEFORE the data
  prior-only vs posterior plots  did the data move it at all?
  leave-one-out sensitivity      per clock model AND per calibration
  uncertainty intervals          reported, not a point estimate

Two disagreements worth knowing by name

Angiosperm crown age

unresolved

Molecular estimates span early Cretaceous to Triassic, against a much younger unambiguous fossil record. Authors disagree over whether rate/calibration artefacts, fossil incompleteness, or both explain the gap.

Placental mammal origin

unresolved

Molecular dates often imply a pre-K–Pg origin; geomolecular calibration modelling yields younger estimates compatible with a radiation near 66 Ma.

And a result that is routinely over-read

One benchmark found RelTime node ages closely comparable to MCMCtree and BEAST — under matched alignment, topology, model and calibration densities. That does not imply agreement when priors or calibrations differ, which is the situation in every real comparison.

You have understood Chapter 8 when…

Five things, without notes

  1. Explain why gene-tree discordance is expected under no gene flow, and what makes it more probable.
  2. State what the anomaly zone is and why it defeats "take the commonest gene tree".
  3. Say precisely what a bootstrap value measures, and why 100% with low gCF is routine.
  4. Explain why concatenation and coalescent disagreeing is information rather than a tie.
  5. List what a published divergence date must expose.
Self-check

A phylogenomic paper reports a concatenated ML tree from 1,847 single-copy orthologs, all nodes at 100% bootstrap, resolving a contentious three-family relationship, and dates the split at 94.3 ± 1.2 Ma with MCMCtree. What is wrong?

support
100% everywhere on 1,847 loci is the expected outcome of concatenation at genome scale — including when the topology is wrong. It is not evidence. Ask for gCF and sCF.
the contentious node
A "contentious" relationship is contentious because the internode is short — precisely the ILS regime where concatenation can be inconsistent. This is the one node where the method is least trustworthy.
one method
No MSC tree, so no way to know whether the conflict is real. Current practice is both.
single-copy filter
1,847 single-copy orthologs is a Chapter 5 selection filter, biased and possibly contaminated by differential loss.
the date
±1.2 Ma is a precision claim, not an accuracy one. It reports posterior width under one calibration set — with no prior-only comparison, no leave-one-out, and no clock-model sensitivity. Given the angiosperm and placental disputes, that interval is not credible as stated.

The tell that ties it together: every number in the abstract is a precision, and none is a robustness.
