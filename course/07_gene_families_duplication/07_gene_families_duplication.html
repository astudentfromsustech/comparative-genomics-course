The answer, first

Four claims stacked; papers establish the fourth

1  the counts are correct       not an assembly or annotation artefact
2  the family is a family      the orthogroup boundary is right
3  it happened on that branch  needs a tree and an ancestral state
4  the change had a consequence   the part everyone writes about

Claim 1 fails by haplotig

In a heterozygous diploid assembled without purging, both haplotypes of a region are assembled separately and every gene there is annotated twice. It looks exactly like a recent tandem expansion.

BUSCO duplicated % settles it cheaply: clean diploid runs 1–3%. A genome at 15% claiming widespread expansion is describing its own assembly.

Fates

Retention is not evidence of selection

FateMechanismFrequency
NonfunctionalizationDisabling mutations → pseudogeneThe overwhelming default
NeofunctionalizationA copy is freed from constraint and gains a functionRare — and the story everyone tells
Subfunctionalization (DDC)Complementary losses; both copies now requiredCommon, and needs no positive selection
Dosage2× product is beneficialCommon for dosage-sensitive genes

Two consequences worth carrying

DDC (Force 1999) showed retention can be entirely neutral-to-degenerative: complementary degenerative mutations in regulatory regions preserve both copies with no adaptive event. Much comparative narrative silently assumes the opposite.

Dosage balance gives a testable prediction: WGD retains dosage-sensitive genes — transcription factors, ribosomal proteins, complex members — because halving one member is deleterious; small-scale duplication retains the reverse (stress, defence, secondary metabolism, chemosensation). So a claimed “adaptive expansion” of transcription factors that turns out tandem rather than WGD-derived is suspect.

4
WGD

Four ways a Ks peak misleads

Saturation

01

Beyond Ks ≈ 1–2 synonymous sites are hit repeatedly; estimates compress and variance inflates. A high-Ks “peak” is often the estimator.

Rate variation

02

The same Ks is a different age in two clades, so peaks cannot be aligned across species to claim a shared WGD.

Tandem contamination

03

A recent family expansion contributes many low-Ks pairs. Tandems are removed first — check whether they were.

Collinearity

the confirmation

WGD leaves syntenic blocks in doubled relationship across chromosomes. A peak without block structure is a hypothesis.

Modelling

What CAFE claims, and how it breaks

CAFE and BadiRate fit a birth–death process for family size along an ultrametric species tree, flagging families improbable under the fitted rate. CAFE 5 allows rate variation across families — a single global λ is badly misspecified for real genomes.

AssumptionBreaks whenConsequence
Family sizes are correctAnnotation pipelines differSpurious rapid families, concentrated in the odd genome
Tree and branch lengths in time are correctConcatenated or uncalibrated treeRates and significance both wrong
Families evolve independentlyTandem arrays, WGDOne event moves many families; counted as independent
One rate fits allAlwaysInflated significance for fast families

The one-minute check

Plot flagged families by species. Concentration in one genome means you are looking at its annotation, not its evolution. CAFE also takes orthogroups as given — every Chapter 3 error enters here as data.

The ladder

What each rung entitles you to say

RungYou may claimIt requires
Candidate“More copies in A than B”Comparable annotation, haplotig purging, BUSCO duplication check
Association“Expanded on branch n”Species tree + ancestral state + birth–death model
Mechanistic“Tandem duplication of locus L”Coordinates, array structure, collinear blocks, Ks among copies
Causal“Contributes to phenotype P”Expression of new copies, divergence consistent with functional change, ideally a perturbation. Cross-species correlation alone does not reach here — Chapter 7

Fallback wording

With rung 1 only you may write “family X shows a higher copy number in A than B”, not “family X is expanded in A”. “Expanded” already asserts rung 2 — a direction, on a branch.

  2. Say why retention is not evidence of positive selection, citing the mechanism.

Self-check

A beetle genome has 214 copies of a detoxification family versus 31 in its sister species; CAFE flags it rapidly evolving (p < 0.001); GO supports “response to xenobiotic stimulus”; the conclusion is insecticide-resistance adaptation. What do you ask for, in order?

1 · haplotigs
BUSCO duplicated % and whether purging was run. 214 vs 31 is the signature of an unpurged heterozygous assembly.
2 · annotation
Same pipeline and same evidence for both genomes?
3 · mechanism
Coordinates. A tandem array is one event, not 183.
4 · distribution
Flagged families across all species — concentrated in this genome?
5 · intactness
How many of the 214 are intact ORFs rather than pseudogenes?

The answer might genuinely be yes — detoxification families really do expand under insecticide pressure. That is exactly why the checks matter: the plausible story is what stops people from running them.
