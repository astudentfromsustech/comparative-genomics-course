Selection inference

A dN/dS result licenses one deliberately narrow sentence. Everything that makes it feel like more — the gene name, the phenotype, the story — comes from somewhere else.

1
The answer, first

The sentence you are entitled to

What a significant result licenses

Under the fitted codon model, the data reject a specified null in favour of an excess of nonsynonymous relative to synonymous substitution, in the tested scope. That is the whole claim.

A gene-wide ω > 1 supports historical diversifying selection averaged across that gene or branch. It does not establish adaptation of the whole gene, a phenotype, a causal amino-acid change, or the time or agent of selection. A branch-site LRT supports ω > 1 at some unobserved codon class on predeclared foreground branches — not every foreground codon or branch. BEB site calls are conditional localisation estimates, not independent confirmations.

The publishable claim, in practice

"Candidate episodic diversifying selection, robust to stated alignment, tree, recombination, composition and multiplicity checks", plus orthogonal biological evidence. Not "proved positive selection".

What the models assume

Synonymous substitutions as a neutral baseline; a correct codon alignment; a correct gene tree; no recombination within the fitted partition; single-nucleotide instantaneous changes; an adequate stationary, reversible mutation model. Violate any of these and you can get an excellent likelihood-ratio statistic with no adaptive amino-acid evolution at all.

3
False positives

Four generators, with measured magnitudes

Recombination

largest

It invalidates the single-tree likelihood factorisation outright.

5% → 90%
site-model type-I error under neutral simulated recombination, at high rates
> 50%
false-positive probability above ρ = 0.024 in one HDV-like setting, rising toward 100%
Detect breakpoints (GARD), partition, fit a tree per partition. This reduces the problem; it does not prove recombinant history removed.

Misalignment

dominant in scans

Indels are innocuous if homologous codons are correctly aligned. Alignment errors produced "unacceptably high" branch-site false positives; PRANK outperformed MUSCLE, MAFFT and ClustalW.

In one empirical branch-site scan, filtering retained only 2,009 of 3,810 (52.7%) initially called sites — an instability measure, not a calibrated true-positive rate.

Paralogy

not preprocessing

Mixed paralogs make columns and branch labels represent a duplication/gene-conversion history rather than the intended orthology history.

Non-allelic conversion simulations increased PAML false positives — worst with high paralog divergence, long converted tracts, and no outgroup. No universal rate exists; it is strongly scenario-dependent.

Multiplicity

arithmetic

At nominal P < 0.05, a 10,000-gene screen has 500 expected null calls before any data or model failure.

State the complete tested family and control FDR across genes, branches, and any post-hoc foreground choices. Selectome corrects across its full branch-site dataset.

A fifth that is not on the classic list — multinucleotide mutations

MNMs can generate branch-site positives as often as the empirical test does under realistic simulated rates, and codons with multiple differences accounted for virtually all support in two genome-scale datasets. The idealised simulations of the 2005 modified test sit at or below nominal 5% — MNMs are outside those simulations.

4
Confounders

Things that raise ω without positive selection

relaxed constraint
Nearly neutral nonsynonymous substitutions fix. A higher mean ω conflates this with intensified selection — which is exactly why RELAX exists. Test it, do not assume it away
small Ne
Chapter 03's Nes again: weaker selection efficacy permits fixation of slightly deleterious changes
gBGC
Favours W→S substitutions, affects neutral flanks and recombination-rich regions. In one primate analysis it could account for >20% of elevated-ω cases, including ω > 2
non-stationary composition
Standard dN, dS and ω all assume equilibrium composition; non-stationarity biases all three. Non-homogeneous codon models correct it

Where the literature genuinely conflicts

On ordinary GC variation: one benchmark found branch-site inference robust but underpowered under GC variation and synonymous saturation, while non-stationary simulations report false selection signals when frequency and selection shifts interact. Both are credible; they test different regimes. Report which one your data resemble rather than citing whichever supports your result.

The gate

Branch-site pre-flight — before the test, not after

run every one of these BEFORE believing an LRT

  1  coding-sequence integrity              no internal stops, no frameshifts
  2  protein-guided codon alignment         + masking SENSITIVITY, not one run
  3  gene tree independently supported      and FOREGROUND DECLARED BEFORE TESTING
  4  recombination screening                GARD; partition; tree per partition
  5  dS / saturation diagnostics            is the synonymous baseline usable?
  6  composition diagnostics                stationarity; consider nonhomogeneous models
  7  repeated-model convergence             multiple starting points — PAML can converge badly
  8  MNM sensitivity                        codons with multiple differences
  9  FDR across the FULL tested family      genes × branches × post-hoc choices
 10  INSPECT THE ACTUAL SUBSTITUTIONS       look at the sites and the alignment columns

Step 3 is the one that quietly invalidates whole papers

A foreground branch chosen after seeing which branches look interesting converts a hypothesis test into a description. The multiplicity correction in step 9 must then include that choice — which is rarely done, and usually cannot be reconstructed afterwards.

You have understood Chapter 11 when…

Five things, without notes

  1. State exactly what a significant branch-site result licenses — and what BEB site calls are.
  2. Name the five false-positive generators with a magnitude for each.
  3. Name four confounders that raise ω without positive selection.
  4. Say why a foreground branch chosen after looking invalidates the test.
  5. Explain why MK and dN/dS are not better and worse versions of each other.
Self-check

A paper reports that TLR4 shows ω = 3.2 on the bat ancestral branch (branch-site, P = 0.004), with 7 BEB sites at posterior > 0.95, three of which fall in the ligand-binding domain — concluding adaptive evolution of innate immunity related to viral tolerance. What do you require?

declared in advance?
Was the bat branch the foreground before testing, or chosen because it looked interesting? If TLR4 was one of many genes and bats one of many branches, what is the full tested family and its FDR?
recombination
TLRs recombine. Unscreened, site-model type-I error can reach 90% — this alone could produce the result.
paralogy
TLRs are a gene family with a duplication history. Is this an orthologous set, verified by synteny, with an outgroup?
alignment
Codon-aware, protein-guided, with masking sensitivity? Half of called sites did not survive filtering in a published scan.
MNMs
Do the 7 BEB sites involve codons with multiple nucleotide differences? If so they may account for essentially all the support.
relaxation
Run RELAX. Bats may show relaxed constraint on TLR4, which raises mean ω without positive selection.
gBGC
Are the substitutions W→S? If so, >20% of elevated-ω primate cases had this explanation.

And the framing check: "three sites in the ligand-binding domain" reads as mechanistic support, but it is post hoc annotation of a statistical result — the domain was not predicted in advance, and ligand-binding domains are large. It belongs on the candidate rung, not the mechanistic one.
