Constraint at scale

What Zoonomia actually delivered

A reference-free 240-placental-mammal Cactus alignment, 131 new assemblies, TOGA orthology annotations, codon alignments and PHAST conservation tracks. At 5% FDR, its phyloP call identifies 3.1% of human bases as constrained.

Two things routinely misstated

phyloP and phastCons are not the same test. phyloP gives basewise rate deviation; phastCons gives element-like calls. And the published primate-wide phastCons analysis uses 43 primates while phyloP uses all 239 — so the two tracks are not even built on the same data.

Constraint is an observability statement. Power depends on branch length, taxon sampling, alignment coverage and neutral-model choice. Zoonomia explicitly reports sitewise species coverage ranging from 1 to 240, with more constraint detected where close relatives are available.

The asymmetry that matters for interpretation

Constraint is not synonymous with function, and non-constraint does not establish neutrality. Rapidly evolving, turnover-prone, lineage-specific, repetitive and unalignable functional sequence is systematically disadvantaged by the test — which is precisely the category Chapter 14 says carries much regulatory function.

The finding to hold onto

No visited primary source demonstrates that any of these has replaced phyloP/phastCons for genome-wide, phylogeny-calibrated constraint or branch-specific selection inference. Claims that they have should be treated as unsupported.

Independent benchmarking found general DNA models underperformed a task-trained CNN on several multispecies and epigenetic tasks. And the Borzoi target-gene result is the sharpest caution available: a model can improve a headline metric while barely beating "pick the nearest TSS" on the question you actually care about.

Structure search is the clearer win. AlphaFold DB plus Foldseek — which encodes local tertiary interactions as a 3Di alphabet — made proteome-scale structural similarity search operational, changing candidate discovery and domain/family assignment. That is Chapter 02's remote-homology problem, genuinely advanced.

The referee checklist, by claim type

Status of this table

There is no validated field-wide checklist specifying minimum evidence separately for these claim types. What follows is a synthesis of published standards, error analyses and documented method limits — assembled across this course, not an endorsed consensus document. Use it as a working standard and say that is what it is.

ClaimMinimum evidenceFallback wording if absent
Gene family expansion
ch 7		 Comparable annotation across genomes · BUSCO duplicated % · mechanism from coordinates and synteny · ancestral state on a dated tree · flagged families not concentrated in one genome "family X shows a higher copy number in A than B"
Gene loss
ch 6		 Conserved flanking syntelogs defining one orthologous interval · direct DNA search of that interval · search of unannotated sequence · read/assembly evidence excluding a gap · outgroup polarisation "no intact copy was detected in the assembly of B"
Positive selection
ch 11		 The ten-item pre-flight — foreground declared in advance, codon-aware alignment with masking sensitivity, recombination screening, dS/saturation and composition diagnostics, MNM sensitivity, RELAX, FDR over the full tested family, sites inspected "candidate episodic diversifying selection, robust to the stated checks"
Introgression
ch 12		 Site-pattern asymmetry and the branch-length signature · ghost-donor consideration · window-variance control · fd rather than D for localisation "the data reject a no-gene-flow model for this quartet". Positive D alone establishes neither donor, recipient, timing, nor tract boundaries
Convergent adaptation
ch 13		 Posterior distribution of independent origins · matched phenotype-specific null · hemiplasy diagnostics on local genealogies · relaxed-constraint alternative tested · functional measurement "convergent substitutions were observed at these sites"
Fixed structural variant
ch 10		 Graph build, version, parameters and assembly order stated · breakpoints validated independently (long reads, second assembly, optical map) · more than one individual per species · merging thresholds and sequence harmonisation reported "an SV differing between these assemblies", not "fixed between species"
Regulatory divergence
ch 14		 Orthology-aware uniform annotation · composition and stage matching from expression, not chronology · species not confounded with batch · cis/trans separated (hybrid design) · assay establishing more than reporter activity "expression differs between these samples"

Practice

Reproducibility, and its honest limit

The limit worth stating out loud

Containers and workflow engines improve repeatability. They cannot make a biologically under-specified analysis valid. A perfectly containerised branch-site scan with a post-hoc foreground is exactly as wrong as an uncontainerised one, and easier to rerun.

No comparative-genomics-specific survey of how many published pipelines are truly rerunnable was verified — so treat confident claims about the field's reproducibility rate, in either direction, as unsupported.

A clade-specific disagreement to know

ANI species boundaries. The 95–96% threshold remains operationally useful, while GTDB cautions that global genome comparisons do not show a clean universal gap. Both positions are held by credible sources; the threshold is a convention that works, not a discovered boundary.

The compressed version

One page, if you keep nothing else

the six questions, in order, for any comparative claim

F1  are the objects comparable?        same annotation? purged? screened?
F2  is it the same entity?             orthologs, not best hits; synteny checked
F3  which lineage, and when?           declared in advance; discordance quantified
F4  what does architecture do?         breakpoints validated; graph stated
F5  does it depart from neutral?       against WHICH null? demography included?
F6  does function follow?              an assay, or a fallback wording

and the one habit

  the boring explanation is nearly always an artefact,
  and nearly always cheaper to test.  So it goes first.

The mark of someone who knows this field is not that they can run the tools. It is that when shown a result they can name the failure mode before they name the method — and that when their own result is beautiful, they run the cheap check anyway.

✓
Acceptance test

The final test

Given a manuscript sentence, you can…

  1. Name the claim type and the chapter that governs it.
  2. State the minimum evidence it requires.
  3. Give the exact weaker wording it falls back to if that evidence is absent.
  4. Say which single check you would ask for first, and why it is the cheapest.
  5. Distinguish a genuine frontier advance from a headline metric — e.g. why Borzoi's eQTL AUROC does not mean it identifies target genes.
The whole course, in one manuscript abstract

"We assembled the genome of X, identified 214 expanded gene families and 47 losses relative to its sister species, detected 62 genes under positive selection on the X branch, found evidence of introgression from Y, and identified convergent substitutions shared with the distantly related Z — together revealing the genomic basis of X's adaptation to high altitude."

Every clause is a separate claim at a separate rung, and the abstract joins them with "together revealing", which is not an inference.

one assembly
Every downstream claim rests on one genome. Ask F1 first: BUSCO duplicated %, contamination screen, annotation parity with the sister species. This single answer can invalidate all five clauses at once — which is why it is the cheapest question in the abstract.
214 + 47
Copy-number and absence claims — ch 7 and ch 6 standards. Mechanism? Syntenic intervals? Or annotation asymmetry producing both simultaneously?
62 selected
ch 11. Foreground declared? Recombination screened? MNMs? RELAX? FDR over how many tests — and does that family include the branch choice?
introgression
ch 12. D or fd? Branch-length signature? Ghost donors excluded? And note it sits awkwardly with the selection claim: introgressed regions have different genealogies, which is a ch 11 assumption violation.
convergence with Z
ch 13. How many independent origins of high altitude? Hemiplasy checked? Matched null? Relaxed constraint excluded?
"adaptation to high altitude"
ch 9 and ch 15. Altitude is one environmental axis among many correlated ones, and the species count is not the effective sample size. No functional evidence is offered for any of it.

The honest version of that abstract ends: "…providing candidate loci for X's high-altitude phenotype, which remain to be functionally tested." That sentence is publishable, defensible, and describes the same work.
