The answer, first

Comparability is not quality

Two excellent assemblies can be uncomparable; two mediocre ones can be perfectly comparable. What matters is whether the differences you measure are differences in biology or in how the two objects were produced.

Instruments

Four metrics, four blind spots

MetricMeasuresBlind to
BUSCOExpected conserved single-copy gene content Bases, phasing, repeats, structure, contamination. Samples conserved orthologs — silent on lineage-specific genes
compleasmThe same quantity via miniprot — faster, and it shifts the numbers Everything BUSCO is. Not an independent evidence class — agreement with BUSCO is not corroboration
MerquryRead k-mers: QV, completeness, phasing Chromosome structure; anything absent from the reads
LAIIntact LTR continuity — repeat-space quality Gene space. Informative mainly for LTR-rich genomes, so it travels badly across clades

Ask for BUSCO duplicated % first

Clean diploid runs 1–3%. A genome reporting 15% duplicated alongside “widespread gene family expansion” is describing its own assembly. It costs nothing and resolves a large share of expansion claims — though it raises the question rather than settling it, since duplicated BUSCOs signal haplotypic or real duplication.

4
The harder half

Annotation is where comparability breaks

Assembly problems yield to better data. Annotation problems do not, because annotation is a modelling choice and pipelines make different ones.

BRAKER3

evidence

GeneMark-ETP + AUGUSTUS over RNA-seq and protein evidence; TSEBRA selects models by extrinsic support.

Helixer

learned

Cross-species deep-learning/HMM ab initio — no per-species evidence needed, which is why it is both attractive and different.

EGAPx

evidence

NCBI's open pipeline: protein and RNA alignment, then Gnomon plus HMM prediction.

Tiberius

newest

The 2024 deep-learning shift, multi-clade in 2026. The direction of travel.

One pipeline for every genome — necessary, not sufficient

Do it. But there is still no community-wide annotation standard (2025), uniform annotation does not equalise unlike evidence — species-specific RNA-seq versus protein-only prediction — and it systematically misses fast-evolving and de novo loci, which is exactly what many comparative papers hunt.

A 2025 benchmark showed established annotation sources still produce materially different orthology results. The choice propagates into Chapter 3.

5
Contamination

The defect that manufactures lineages

The scale

measured

FCS-GX found 36.8 Gbp of suspected contamination across 1.6 million GenBank assemblies — 0.16% overall, but half of it from 161 assemblies.

Contamination is concentrated, not smeared. An aggregate rate says nothing about your genome.
Astashyn et al. 2024

The casualty

overturned

A 2026 reanalysis concluded contamination substantially undermined a 2021 claim of cross-phylum antibiotic-resistance dissemination.

The claim fell to a reference screen, not to better biology.
Godron et al. 2026 · code public

Resolve at the locus, not by default

FCS-GX's authors report no excess calls in genomes with high-confidence lateral transfer; the 2026 reanalysis shows a contaminated reference can overturn a specific HGT conclusion. Both hold. So validate per locus — raw-read coverage across the candidate and its junctions, contig context, taxonomic screening, an independent assembly — rather than defaulting to either HGT or contamination.

Open question: there is no audited estimate of how much older comparative literature changes under modern screening. The FCS-GX aggregate is sometimes cited as though it were that estimate. It is not.

Acceptance test

You have understood Chapter 2 when…

Five things, without notes

  1. Name the false biology each defect produces — haplotigs, collapsed repeats, fragmentation, misjoin, contamination.
  2. Say why high contiguity ≠ high correctness.
  3. State what BUSCO, Merqury and LAI are blind to, and why compleasm agreeing with BUSCO is not corroboration.
  4. Explain why one annotation pipeline is necessary but not sufficient.
  5. Give the resolution procedure for an HGT claim without defaulting either way.
Self-check

A manuscript reports 41 genes in a new insect genome with no homolog outside the family, enriched for detoxification domains, several adjacent to bacterial-like sequence — concluding horizontal acquisition then lineage-specific radiation. What do you ask for?

1 · screen
FCS-GX on the assembly as submitted. “Adjacent to bacterial-like sequence” is the signature of a contaminant contig, not of transfer.
2 · coverage
Raw reads across the candidate loci and their junctions. A real insertion has continuous insect-read coverage running into flanking insect sequence; a contaminant contig does not.
3 · provenance
Were the comparison genomes annotated the same way? “No homolog outside the family” is the most annotation-sensitive claim available — see the 15× result.
4 · duplication
BUSCO duplicated %, before accepting “radiation”.
5 · replication
An independent assembly of the same species.

Each check is cheaper than the claim it would displace, and any of the first three could explain the entire result on its own.
