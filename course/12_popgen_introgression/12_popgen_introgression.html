Population history and introgression

Most scan statistics measure departure from a simplistic neutral, panmictic baseline — not selection. Demography and gene flow are therefore not confounders at the edges. They are the default explanation.

Question
How do demography and gene flow masquerade as adaptation?

1
The answer, first

Why the mimicry is structural

A bottleneck, range expansion or unmodelled structure changes allele-frequency variance, and can make neutral loci extreme in FST. Background selection reduces linked neutral diversity most strongly in low-recombination, gene-dense regions — inflating relative differentiation with no locus-specific barrier to gene flow anywhere.

The single most useful rule in this chapter

A high-FST region without elevated dXY is not evidence for reduced gene flow. Background selection and recurrent selection create high-FST, low-π "islands" on their own.

FST is a relative measure. Reduce within-population diversity and it rises without any change in between-population divergence. That is what BGS does.

2
The signature

ILS is symmetric; introgression is not

What a significant D licenses

Evidence against a narrow no-gene-flow quartet null. Not an identified donor, not a direction, not a date, and certainly not an adaptive function.

Demography

Resolution is measured in coalescent time, not calendar years

Why "PSMC works from 20 ka to 3 Ma" is not a portable fact

That was PSMC's original human validation. What is actually observable is set by mutation rate, recombination map, sample size, phasing, genome length, recent growth, bottlenecks and migration. Periods with few coalescences are intrinsically invisible — in any species.

Use these to build nulls, not histories

All of them return coalescent-rate histories, confounded by structure and gene flow. The productive use is to generate competing, simulation-calibrated nulls — not to declare one true demographic history and then scan against it.

5
The standard

What a credible selection scan requires

the null must jointly simulate

  the fitted demographic graph          not a constant-size population
  recombination and mutation-rate variation
  ascertainment and calling filters     Chapter 3's SFS problem
  BGS — or a matched low-recombination baseline

and the report must show

  F_ST   WITH  π,  d_XY,  LD / haplotype structure,  replicate contrasts

an F_ST tail alone is not a result. It is one number from a relative
measure, with at least four non-adaptive explanations available.

Why replicate contrasts matter most

Independent population pairs that experienced the same environmental transition are the closest thing to a natural experiment available here. A peak that recurs across independent contrasts survives most of the demographic explanations at once — which no amount of single-contrast statistics can do.

Acceptance test

You have understood Chapter 12 when…

Five things, without notes

  1. Give the statistical signature separating ILS from introgression — both parts.
  2. Name five conditions under which that signature fails.
  3. State what a significant D does and does not identify.
  4. Explain why high FST without elevated dXY is not evidence of reduced gene flow.
  5. Say why demographic inference should build nulls rather than histories.
Self-check

A paper reports D = 0.08 (Z = 4.1) between a highland and a lowland species pair and a third relative, identifies twelve high-fd windows, notes one contains a haemoglobin gene with elevated FST, and concludes adaptive introgression of a high-altitude haemoglobin variant. What do you require?

1 · direction & donor
D establishes non-zero gene flow somewhere in the quartet. It does not say which lineage donated, or whether an unsampled ghost did. Ask what excludes a ghost donor.
2 · branch lengths
Do the discordant loci show the shorter internal branches expected of introgression, or symmetric distributions consistent with ILS? Without QuIBL-style evidence, D alone cannot separate them.
3 · window variance
D has high variance in short windows and is not a local proportion estimator. Twelve windows out of how many tested, and with what genome-wide FDR?
4 · the FST peak
Is dXY elevated there? If not, BGS in a low-recombination region explains it without gene flow — and haemoglobin loci are often in gene-dense regions.
5 · "adaptive"
Nothing above tests adaptation. Introgression plus a gene with a plausible name is candidate-rung evidence. Selection on the introgressed haplotype needs its own test, and function needs an assay.

Note the compounding: the haemoglobin gene was not predicted in advance — it was found inside a window and then named. That is Chapter 11's post-hoc annotation problem appearing in a different statistic.
