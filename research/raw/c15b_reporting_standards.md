## Answer

The minimum reportable record is a claim-specific, versioned provenance bundle: biological samples and metadata; raw reads and accessions; assembly/annotation/reference versions and quality diagnostics; orthology/alignment/tree or population-genetic inputs; exact model, parameters, and multiple-testing treatment; uncertainty/sensitivity analyses; locus-level evidence behind every headline result; and archived code, environments, and inputs sufficient to regenerate tables and figures. MIxS supplies baseline genome/sample contextual metadata, while journal policies require data, materials, code, and analytical-design disclosure; neither is a complete comparative-genomics claim checklist. [MIxS](https://github.com/GenomicsStandardsConsortium/mixs) [Nature Genetics policy](https://www.nature.com/ng/editorial-policies/reporting-standards)

A referee should reject evolutionary verbs when the paper reports only a tool output. “Expansion,” “loss,” “positive selection,” “introgression,” “convergence,” and “fixation” are model-dependent inferences, not direct observations. Require an independently inspectable chain from reads/assemblies through homology or variants to the fitted evolutionary model, with alternatives that address the principal confounder. Assembly fragmentation, gene-model error, reference bias, alignment error, ancestral structure, incomplete lineage sorting (ILS), and ascertainment are recurring causes of overturned claims. [Han et al. 2013](https://academic.oup.com/mbe/article/30/8/1987/1017616) [Mallick et al. 2009](https://reich.hms.harvard.edu/sites/reich.hms.harvard.edu/files/inline-files/2009_GenomeResearch_Mallick_falsepositives_selection.pdf) [Rhodin et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9516821/)

“Reproducible” should mean a tagged, archived workflow run is possible—not merely that scripts are online. Nextflow/Snakemake plus pinned containers help portability, but containers do not preserve mutable reference data, sample manifests, random seeds, or biological decisions. nf-core provides unusually strong release/testing/container conventions, but it is not a comparative-genomics standard and I found no comparative-genomics-specific survey estimating what fraction of papers ship runnable end-to-end pipelines. [Wratten et al. 2021](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41592-021-01254-9) [nf-core](https://nf-co.re/docs/nf-core-tools) [survey result: unverified]

Standards diverge by clade. Microbial work normally makes strain selection, assembly completeness/contamination, ANI/alignment fraction, GTDB release, and pangenome clustering explicit. Plant work additionally needs phased subgenomes, repeat-library provenance, syntenic anchors, and WGD/homeolog discrimination. Animal work increasingly demands chromosome-scale phased references, sex of the sampled individual, and population-level validation for SV claims. These practices should not be transferred mechanically: 95% ANI is not a plant/animal species criterion, and bacterial presence/absence matrices cannot resolve plant homeologs. [GTDB](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8728215/) [VGP](https://vertebrategenomesproject.org/technology/)

## Key findings

- **Universal record.** Demand accessioned raw data; specimen/strain identifiers, geography/date and sequencing metadata; assembly and annotation release/checks; all genome/protein inputs; reference database release; exact commands/configuration/seeds; intermediate claim tables; and a DOI-archived code release plus container digests. MIxS explicitly distinguishes required, recommended, and optional fields across genome, MAG, metagenome and marker-gene checklists. [MIxS specification](https://github.com/GenomicsStandardsConsortium/mixs) [MIxS comparative-genomics protocol](https://link.springer.com/protocol/10.1007/978-1-0716-3838-5_20)

- **Gene-family expansion.** Require consistent annotations or evidence that annotation heterogeneity is modelled; orthogroups plus gene trees reconciled to a dated/species tree; copy-number table by locus; assembly/read-depth or synteny confirmation for focal copies; CAFE/BadiRate-style model, error model, branch tests and FDR; and sensitivity to orthology method and excluded poor genomes. Without this, write “larger annotated orthogroup” rather than “lineage-specific expansion/adaptive expansion.” Assembly/annotation error systematically inflates inferred gain/loss rates. [Han, Thomas, Lugo-Martinez & Hahn 2013, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/molbev/mst100) [Dent et al. 2014, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1003998)

- **Gene loss.** Require a syntenic ancestral locus (or whole-genome alignment), search of translated genomic DNA and unannotated contigs/reads, intact orthologs in appropriate outgroups/sisters, and a reconciled gene tree; document pseudogene/partial-loss criteria. Otherwise write “no annotated homolog detected,” not “lost.” A reanalysis found false absences concentrated among species-specific, biologically implausible losses, including false KNL1-complex absences. [Kollmar & Mühlhausen 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6736253/)

- **Positive selection.** Require codon-aware, manually/auditably filtered alignment; orthology/paralogy and recombination screening; foreground branch defined a priori; model and null, site/branch estimates, LRT calibration and genome-wide FDR; and robustness to alignment masking, sequence quality, and alternative models. Demand site-level alignments and functional interpretation as a hypothesis, not proof. Without these, write “elevated dN/dS/model-based candidate,” not “positively selected/adaptive.” Sequence, annotation and alignment errors can generate genome-wide false positives. [Fletcher & Yang 2010, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/molbev/msq115) [Mallick et al. 2009](https://reich.hms.harvard.edu/sites/reich.hms.harvard.edu/files/inline-files/2009_GenomeResearch_Mallick_falsepositives_selection.pdf)

- **Introgression.** Require population sampling rather than one genome per taxon; genotype/callability filters; explicit quartet/outgroup and block-jackknife uncertainty; genome-wide D/f statistics plus local analyses only where calibrated; demographic/model comparison that includes ILS and ancestral structure; and direction/timing stated only if identifiable. Otherwise write “allele-sharing excess consistent with gene flow,” not “introgression from X into Y.” D is useful genome-wide but is not a local-window estimator, and population structure can create false positives. [Martin, Davey & Jiggins 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4271521/) [Hibbins & Hahn 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9208645/)

- **Convergent adaptation.** Require independently evolved phenotype assignments, phylogenetically controlled null simulations, homologous aligned sites/loci, correction for branch-length/composition/rate heterogeneity and multiple testing, plus evidence separating recurrent mutation from introgression/recombination. Functional replication is required for strong causal wording. Otherwise write “repeated association/candidate molecular convergence,” not “convergent adaptation.” Genome-wide scans are especially vulnerable to alignment/orthology error and phenotype non-independence. [Fletcher & Yang 2010, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/molbev/msq115) [Thornton & Jensen 2007, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1534/genetics.106.064642)

- **Structural-variant fixation.** Require breakpoint-resolved calls, caller/technology agreement or orthogonal validation, assembly/read support, genotyping and callability across adequately sampled populations, allele-frequency confidence intervals, and evidence that “fixed” means frequency 1 within stated sampling—not merely absent from a reference. Test reference and mapping bias. Otherwise write “SV observed in sampled genomes” or “high-frequency SV.” Long-read/population work shows that reference incompleteness and short-read ascertainment miss biologically relevant sequence. [Höps et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9745067/) [Reference-genome-choice study, 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0092867425010268)

- **Microbial practice.** Report GTDB-Tk/database release, ANI method and alignment fraction, quality/contamination thresholds, dereplication, plasmid/MAG treatment, pangenome tool/version and gene-clustering thresholds, and sampling design. ANI 95–96% is widely used but GTDB itself reports no universal global discontinuity across 83–95% ANI; it is a taxonomic convention, not a biological law. [Parks et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8728215/)

- **Plant practice.** Report ploidy/heterozygosity, haplotype versus collapsed assembly, TE library/masking and annotation versions, synteny anchors and parameters, Ks model/filtering, and gene-tree support when calling WGD or homeolog loss. Synteny is central because recurrent WGD, rearrangement and translocation make simple orthogroup counts inadequate. [PGDD 2.0, 2025](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/54/D1/D1753/8343508) [Lyons et al. 2008](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1365-313X.2007.03326.x)

- **Animal practice.** Report sampled sex, chromosome/sex-chromosome treatment, phasing, QC and contamination, and validate claimed absence/SVs across individuals. VGP’s reference target—contig N50 ≥1 Mb, scaffold N50 ≥10 Mb, 90% chromosomal placement independently supported, QV40, phased—illustrates why draft-based animal gene-loss claims are weak. [VGP technology standard](https://vertebrategenomesproject.org/technology/) [Rhodin et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9516821/)

- **Documented reversals/failures.** The early human-genome claim of 223 bacterial horizontal transfers was largely reinterpreted as contamination; draft assemblies also cleave genes and create false copies. Later reassessments show that erroneous absence/gain calls can change which families appear expanded or contracted. [Dent et al. 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4256071/) [chimeric-annotation reassessment, 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12220653/)

## What the standard corpus misses

- **Post–February 2025:** Chimeric gene-model errors remain pervasive in non-model eukaryotes and can reverse CAFE-style expansion/contraction assignments; this is a particularly relevant new referee check. [Liu et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12220653/)

- **Post–February 2025:** A gray-fox comparison reports that mapping to heterospecific references changes population-genetic inferences and SV detection; reference choice now deserves explicit sensitivity analysis in non-model animal studies. [reference-choice study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0092867425010268)

- **2025/26 resource update:** PGDD 2.0 provides a curated plant-focused synteny/WGD framework; a 2026 phylogenomic WGD-detection method argues that scalable inference should move beyond synteny alone. [PGDD 2.0](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/54/D1/D1753/8343508) [Kalhor, Lafond & Scornavacca 2026](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/15578666251415567)

- **2026 evidence remains preliminary:** a July 2026 bioRxiv survey examines applied containerization in bioinformatics, but it is a preprint and not comparative-genomics-specific. [preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.07.17.739198v1)

## Caveats and open questions

- I found no validated, field-wide checklist that specifies minimum evidence separately for all six requested comparative claim types; the claim-specific requirements above are a synthesis of standards, error analyses, and method limitations rather than an endorsed single consensus document. [unverified]

- There is a genuine disagreement over ANI: 95–96% remains operationally useful, while GTDB cautions that global genome comparisons do not show a clean universal gap. [GTDB](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8728215/)

- D-statistic literature agrees that ancestral structure/ILS complicate interpretation, but differs over practical robustness under particular demographic models; positive D alone does not establish donor, recipient, timing, or local tract boundaries. [Hibbins & Hahn 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9208645/)

- Containers and workflow engines improve repeatability but cannot make a biologically under-specified analysis valid; no comparative-genomics-specific prevalence survey of truly rerunnable paper pipelines was verified. [Wratten et al. 2021](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41592-021-01254-9)
