## Answer

Comparative genomics is usually taught as a *workflow plus foundations*, not as a single research-field survey. Across the public curricula reviewed, the recurring dependency order is: data/sequence technology and file formats → alignment/search and evolutionary models → assembly, QC and annotation → homology/orthology, synteny and genome comparison → phylogeny/population history/selection → functional or phenotype interpretation. Workshops put Unix, reproducibility, and a runnable pipeline even earlier; theory courses put alignment algorithms, substitution models, likelihood, and phylogeny first. [Bologna](https://www.unibo.it/en/study/course-units-transferable-skills-moocs/course-unit-catalogue/course-unit/2025/471384), [Stanford CS262](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs262/cgi-bin/index.php), [Evomics 2026](https://evomics.org/workshops/genomics/2026)

Your main weakness is therefore a missing prerequisite layer, not lack of advanced topics. Chapters 3–10 assume the learner can judge an alignment, substitution model, likelihood calculation, annotation evidence chain, and VCF/calling pipeline, but none explicitly teaches those. A referee-level course needs those methods because their assumptions determine whether later orthology, dN/dS, phylogenomics, pangenome, and convergence claims are interpretable. [MIT 6.047](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-047-computational-biology-fall-2015/pages/syllabus/), [UMD CMSC498Y](https://www.cs.umd.edu/class/spring2022/cmsc498Y/index.html)

Real courses also treat functional annotation, data resources, and reproducible computation as first-class material. Bologna explicitly includes public databases, profile HMMs, GO annotation/enrichment, shell/data integrity/Conda, and a public-data project; EMBL-EBI teaches Ensembl Compara, VEP, BioMart, and data upload. [Bologna](https://www.unibo.it/en/study/course-units-transferable-skills-moocs/course-unit-catalogue/course-unit/2025/471384), [EMBL-EBI Ensembl course](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/training/online/courses/ensembl-browser-series/)

There is no single canonical pipeline. The applied curricula converge on one small eukaryote or population dataset taken from reads through QC, assembly or mapping, annotation, variants/SVs, and comparison; evolutionary-genomics courses instead use VCF/SNP datasets through PCA, phylogenomics, introgression, scans, selection, and synteny. [Physalia](https://www.physalia-courses.org/courses-workshops/course34/), [Wellcome Sanger biodiversity materials](https://github.com/rapidspeciation/biodiversity_genomics_course)

## Key findings

- In this sample, alignment is a prerequisite rather than a late application: Stanford teaches pairwise/multiple alignment, HMMs, probabilistic interpretation, then comparative-genomics applications; MIT likewise lists sequence analysis before comparative genomics and phylogenomics. [Stanford CS262](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs262/cgi-bin/index.php), [MIT 6.047](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-047-computational-biology-fall-2015/pages/syllabus/)
- The modern practical spine is explicitly technological: Evomics 2026 ordered sequencing → Unix/file types → alignment → assembly/curation → variant calling → annotation → SV/population/comparative genomics → phylogenomics and adaptation. [Evomics 2026 schedule](https://evomics.org/workshops/genomics/2026)
- Proper functional interpretation is commonly operationalized as annotation evidence, domains/profiles, Gene Ontology annotation/enrichment, browsers, and database export—not merely “non-coding comparison.” [Bologna](https://www.unibo.it/en/study/course-units-transferable-skills-moocs/course-unit-catalogue/course-unit/2025/471384), [EMBL-EBI](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/training/online/courses/ensembl-browser-series/)
- Microbial/ecological genomics is usually a separate track: recent workshops include metagenomics/metatranscriptomics, while the current comparative-genomics methods volume has dedicated prokaryotic-gene-family, pangenome-core, and bacterial-AMR chapters. [Evomics](https://evomics.org/workshops/genomics/2026), [Comparative Genomics: Methods and Protocols, 2e](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-0716-3838-5)
- Research communication is unusually explicit in MIT 6.047: NIH-style proposal, peer review, independent project, conference presentation, and journal-style paper. Add this as assessed practice, not a final checklist. [MIT 6.047](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-047-computational-biology-fall-2015/pages/syllabus/)
- Useful current reference set: Lesk, *Introduction to Bioinformatics*, 5e; Lesk, *Introduction to Genomics*, 3e; Compeau & Pevzner, *Bioinformatics Algorithms*, 3e; Anisimova (ed.), *Evolutionary Genomics*, 2e; Yang, *Molecular Evolution: A Statistical Approach*. [OUP Lesk](https://learninglink.oup.com/access/leskbioinf5e), [Bologna bibliography](https://www.unibo.it/en/study/course-units-transferable-skills-moocs/course-unit-catalogue/course-unit/2025/471384), [Compeau/Pevzner](https://www.bioinformaticsalgorithms.org/product-page/bioinformatics-algorithms-3rd-edition), [Anisimova](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4939-9074-0), [Yang](https://academic.oup.com/book/26340)
- The notable 2025–26 shift is visible, but not yet universal: pangenome graphs, long-read SV/assembly curation, single-cell/spatial genomics, and LLMs appear in current workshop material; Ensembl’s browser course was reviewed in March 2026. [EMBL-EBI pangenome webinar](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/training/events/pangenome-graphs-new-paradigm-comparative-genomics), [Evomics 2026](https://evomics.org/workshops/genomics/2026), [Ensembl course](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/training/online/courses/ensembl-browser-series/)

## What my 14 chapters are MISSING

1. **A new Chapter −1: sequence-comparison and statistical-model foundations.** Teach dynamic programming/local-global alignment, scoring/substitution matrices, BLAST significance/compositional bias, MSA uncertainty, profiles/HMMs, and likelihood on trees. This is the largest omission because it underwrites orthology, WGA, phylogenomics, conservation and codon models. Put it before Chapters 1–3. [Stanford CS262](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs262/cgi-bin/index.php), [Bologna](https://www.unibo.it/en/study/course-units-transferable-skills-moocs/course-unit-catalogue/course-unit/2025/471384), [Yang](https://academic.oup.com/book/26340)

2. **A formal molecular-evolution/population-genetics foundation.** Neutral and nearly-neutral theory, mutation/recombination/drift/linked selection, coalescent basics, allele-frequency spectra, LD and genotype likelihoods should precede species trees, selection and introgression. Your Chapter 6 contains some downstream results but not the shared generative theory. Put this between Chapters 1 and 6. [MIT 6.047](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-047-computational-biology-fall-2015/pages/syllabus/), [Yang](https://academic.oup.com/book/26340)

3. **Functional annotation and enrichment as an evidence problem.** Add gene prediction evidence, domains/profile HMMs, ontology provenance, GO-enrichment null models/multiple testing, annotation-transfer bias, and database versioning. Split between Chapter 2 and a new bridge before Chapter 12. [Bologna](https://www.unibo.it/en/study/course-units-transferable-skills-moocs/course-unit-catalogue/course-unit/2025/471384)

4. **Data resources and genome-browser literacy.** Require hands-on Ensembl Compara, UCSC, NCBI/RefSeq, UniProt/InterPro/Pfam, OrthoDB/eggNOG, BioMart/API and coordinate/build/version discipline. Put this within Chapter 2, then reuse it throughout. [EMBL-EBI](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/training/online/courses/ensembl-browser-series/), [Bologna](https://www.unibo.it/en/study/course-units-transferable-skills-moocs/course-unit-catalogue/course-unit/2025/471384)

5. **A distinct microbial/viral comparative-genomics branch.** Cover horizontal transfer/recombination, operons, mobile elements/phages, ANI/species concepts, core/accessory pangenomes, gene-content phylogeny, and microbial metagenomics. These cannot be treated as a simple special case of eukaryotic synteny/WGD. Add a fork after Chapters 3–5. [Comparative Genomics: Methods and Protocols, 2e](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-0716-3838-5), [Evomics](https://evomics.org/workshops/genomics/2026)

6. **Reproducible research and study design.** Include FASTQ/BAM/VCF/GFF semantics, metadata, checksums, workflow managers, containers/Conda, Git, parameter provenance, compute cost, and pre-specified contrasts. Make it a running lab standard from Chapter 0, with a capstone. [Bologna](https://www.unibo.it/en/study/course-units-transferable-skills-moocs/course-unit-catalogue/course-unit/2025/471384), [MIT 6.047](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-047-computational-biology-fall-2015/pages/syllabus/)

7. **Comparative functional genomics beyond sequence.** Comparative transcript isoforms, expression normalization/design, chromatin/regulatory assays, single-cell/spatial comparability, and the limitations of cross-species cell-type mapping belong in Chapter 12. [Stanford CS273A](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs273a/aut2015.html), [Evomics](https://evomics.org/workshops/genomics/2026)

8. **Genome architecture as a coherent topic.** Add transposable-element life cycles, tandem/rDNA repeats, centromeres/telomeres, recombination landscapes, chromosome fusions/fissions, and 3D/chromatin architecture. Distribute across Chapters 4, 5, 8 and 12 rather than treating repeats only as QC. [Physalia adaptation-genomics programme](https://www.physalia-courses.org/courses-workshops/courseadaptationgenomics/), [Anisimova](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4939-9074-0)

Ancient DNA is a valuable elective case study for damage, contamination and time-aware inference, but not core comparative genomics; metagenomics is core only if microbial/ecological genomics is in scope. [Stanford CS273A](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs273a/aut2015.html), [Evomics](https://evomics.org/workshops/genomics/2026)

## What my chapters have that courses typically DON'T

- Your “six nested framework layers,” evidence ladder, and referee checklist are a distinctive synthesis; the reviewed curricula teach pieces, not an explicit cross-method audit framework. [Harvard OEB 275R](https://beta.my.harvard.edu/course/OEB275R/2025-Fall/001), [MIT 6.047](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-047-computational-biology-fall-2015/pages/syllabus/)
- The tight integration of orthology, synteny/ancestral reconstruction, WGD/dosage balance, MSC/ILS, PGLS/RER, selection, introgression and convergence is more research-survey-like than typical broad genomics courses. Advanced short courses isolate some of these tools instead. [Physalia adaptation genomics](https://www.physalia-courses.org/courses-workshops/courseadaptationgenomics/), [Harvard OEB 275R](https://beta.my.harvard.edu/course/OEB275R/2025-Fall/001)
- Explicit treatment of “N species ≠ N data points,” model criticism, and adaptive explanation is unusually strong; preserve it, but move the necessary statistical and molecular-evolution material earlier. [Harvard OEB 275R](https://beta.my.harvard.edu/course/OEB275R/2025-Fall/001), [Yang](https://academic.oup.com/book/26340)

## Caveats and open questions

- MIT, Stanford and edX pages are archival, so they establish teaching lineage rather than current offerings. Their public syllabi should not be read as 2026 catalogs. [MIT 6.047](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-047-computational-biology-fall-2015/pages/syllabus/), [Stanford CS273A](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs273a/aut2015.html), [edX syllabus](https://courses.edx.org/asset-v1%3AUSMx%2BBIF001x%2B2T2017%2Btype%40asset%2Bblock/BIF001x_Syllabus.pdf)
- Harvard OEB 275R’s description is public but its detailed weekly syllabus is not; I have not inferred a lecture list. [Harvard catalogue](https://beta.my.harvard.edu/course/OEB275R/2025-Fall/001)
- I did not find a public, detailed syllabus for a current CSHL comparative-genomics course or a dedicated Wellcome Connecting Science comparative-genomics course; this is a visibility limitation, not evidence that they teach nothing in the area. [unverified]
- Sources disagree mainly by framing: computational-biology courses foreground algorithms/statistics; genomics workshops foreground sequencing-to-result operations; evolutionary-biology courses foreground phylogeny, trait evolution and genotype–phenotype explanation. The appropriate syllabus therefore depends on whether your course’s primary identity is method-building, analysis practice, or evolutionary inference. [Stanford CS262](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs262/cgi-bin/index.php), [Physalia](https://www.physalia-courses.org/courses-workshops/course34/), [Harvard OEB 275R](https://beta.my.harvard.edu/course/OEB275R/2025-Fall/001)
