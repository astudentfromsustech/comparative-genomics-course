## Answer

A comparative genomicist needs a generative model, not a catalogue of tests: mutations arise with biased, heterogeneous rates; their fates are shaped by drift, direct selection, recombination, gene conversion, demography, and selection at linked sites; and the observed genome is a correlated set of local genealogies rather than independent loci. Under this view, a dN/dS estimate, F_ST peak, discordant gene tree, or fixed inversion is a statistic generated by several competing histories—not evidence of adaptation, isolation, introgression, or functional importance by itself.

“Neutral” is the indispensable null because it supplies explicit predictions for diversity, the site-frequency spectrum (SFS), linkage disequilibrium (LD), fixation, and gene-tree variation. It is not a claim that biology is neutral. Kimura’s theory proposed that most molecular substitutions are neutral; Ohta’s nearly-neutral theory adds mutations whose fate depends on the product of selection strength and effective population size, so the same mutation can behave nearly neutrally in one lineage and be efficiently selected in another. The unresolved debate is therefore largely empirical and definitional: how much polymorphism/substitution is directly adaptive, how much is weakly deleterious, and how much apparently “neutral” variation is indirectly affected by linked selection. Recent reviewers explicitly warn that neither original nor nearly-neutral theory is identical to the strict-neutrality null used in tests. [Neutralist/selectionist review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37621151/)

The practical null must be locus- and species-specific. Mutation-rate and spectrum variation can mimic rate shifts; recombination both breaks associations and changes the reach of linked selection; GC-biased gene conversion (gBGC) can mimic positive selection and alter substitution patterns; bottlenecks, expansion, migration, and structure reshape the SFS and LD genome-wide; sweeps and background selection reshape local genealogies. Thus, interpret dN/dS against codon-model, mutation-spectrum, gBGC, and Ne expectations; F_ST against demographic structure and heterogeneous diversity; gene-tree discordance first as incomplete lineage sorting (ILS), then migration; and fixed structural variants against mutation bias, drift, recombination suppression, and ascertainment before invoking adaptation.

The coalescent is the unifying language: follow sampled lineages backward until they merge, place mutations on their branches, and—once recombination is admitted—allow the genealogy to change along the chromosome, producing an ancestral recombination graph (ARG). Chapter 03 should teach readers to ask: *which assumed genealogy, sampling process, and error model would generate this signal without the headline biological explanation?*

## Key findings

- The strict neutral model predicts molecular evolution from neutral mutation plus drift; its classic rate result is that the neutral substitution rate equals the neutral mutation rate, rather than census population size. [Kimura 1968](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/5637732/)

- Nearly-neutral theory concerns weakly selected mutations whose fate varies with Ne (more exactly, scaled selection such as 2Ne*s* or 4Ne*s*, depending on convention and ploidy), so cross-lineage differences in Ne can alter both polymorphism and protein-evolution rates. [Ohta 1973 record](https://cir.nii.ac.jp/crid/1361137044748183808) [nearly-deleterious model analysis](https://academic.oup.com/mbe/article/19/12/2142/997518)

- Do not teach “neutral theory = null” without qualification: a recent review argues that the original, revised, and nearly-neutral theories are substantive hypotheses and none is the strict-neutrality null; this distinction is central to the still-active neutralist/selectionist dispute. [de Jong et al. review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37621151/)

- The defensible modern middle position is not “selection versus neutrality,” but that direct selection, weak selection, drift, and linked selection all contribute, with their relative importance varying by taxon, genomic region, and question. Claims about the *proportion* of adaptive substitutions remain model-dependent because demography, the distribution of fitness effects, gBGC, and linkage confound them. [half-century debate review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10839204/)

- Mutation is not a homogeneous clock: directly counting de novo mutations in pedigrees yielded human estimates roughly twofold below older indirect evolutionary estimates, materially changing human divergence-time interpretations and raising the possibility of mutation-rate evolution. [Campbell & Eichler review](https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev-genom-031714-125740) If ignored, rate and spectrum heterogeneity can be misread as lineage-specific selection, accelerated evolution, or altered divergence time.

- Ne is the size of an idealized population that matches a specified genetic process—coalescence, drift, inbreeding, variance in allele-frequency change—not a headcount. It may vary through time, across genomic regions, and by estimator; skewed reproductive success, sex ratio, age structure, migration, and linked selection all matter. [Waples 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37873672/) [Ne/N review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38721593/) Treating Ne as census size can miscalibrate drift, mutation-scaled parameters, selection efficacy, and divergence dates.

- Recombination reduces LD and lets local genealogies differ. LD is non-random allelic association; quantities such as D and r² depend on recombination, drift, mutation, selection, population history, and structure. [Slatkin review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5124487/) Ignoring its heterogeneous rate makes both sweep signals and demographic inferences appear more spatially coherent or independent than they are.

- Gene conversion is a short, non-reciprocal repair-associated transfer process. gBGC preferentially transmits GC over AT alleles in many eukaryotes and can drive even deleterious GC alleles upward or to fixation, thereby mimicking positive selection—including at coding sites. [Duret & Galtier 2009](https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev-genom-082908-150001)

- Background selection (purifying selection on linked deleterious alleles) reduces linked neutral diversity and alters coalescence times; classic selective sweeps similarly reduce nearby diversity through hitchhiking. [Charlesworth, Morgan & Charlesworth 1993](https://academic.oup.com/genetics/article/134/4/1289/6011194) [Maynard Smith & Haigh 1974](https://cir.nii.ac.jp/crid/1360574093459420928) Ignoring either can turn low diversity, high F_ST, or an SFS distortion into a false demographic event or positive-selection outlier.

- The size of this problem can be substantial: in one human analysis, BGS and gBGC jointly affected up to 95% of variants and biased demographic inference even at synonymous and non-transcribed sites. That is a human-specific result, not a universal percentage. [Pouyet et al. 2018](https://elifesciences.org/articles/36317)

- The standard neutral coalescent is drift viewed backward from a sample: lineages choose ancestors until coalescing at an MRCA, then mutations on the random genealogy generate polymorphism. [Rosenberg & Nordborg 2002](https://www.nature.com/articles/nrg795) Its elementary form assumes a panmictic, randomly mating Wright–Fisher-like population of constant size, neutral nonrecombining loci, and usually simple mutation assumptions; departures require structured, variable-size, selective, or recombining extensions. [Wakeley chapter](https://wakeleylab.oeb.harvard.edu/sites/g/files/omnuum5776/files/wakeleylab/files/wakeleychapterlongdraft.pdf)

- The SFS counts derived alleles by sample frequency. Expansion tends to produce excess rare variants; bottlenecks, structure, and selection can each distort it, often non-identifiably. Consequently, an SFS departure is evidence against a *specified* neutral-demographic model, not a selection call. [selection–demography review](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/j.1365-294x.2011.05308.x) [linked sweeps and inference](https://academic.oup.com/genetics/article/204/3/1207/6066358)

- The multispecies coalescent (MSC) embeds within-population coalescence inside species-tree branches. It predicts gene-tree discordance through ILS when ancestral lineages fail to coalesce on short internal branches; discordance is therefore expected under no gene flow. [Degnan & Rosenberg 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19307040/) Chapter 08 should distinguish ILS from introgression, which needs additional evidence and models.

- Recombination breaks the single-tree assumption: an ARG is the correlated sequence of local genealogies along a genome. It is conceptually the right object for joint inference of demography, selection, and ancestry, but estimated ARGs remain model-based and uncertain. [Nielsen, Vaughn & Deng 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41576-024-00772-4)

- The field is moving toward ARG-aware analysis, not abandoning summary statistics outright. Relate estimates genome-wide local trees for thousands of samples; tsinfer/tskit infer and store correlated genealogies; ARG-Needle scaled ARG inference to 337,464 UK Biobank samples. [Relate documentation](https://myersgroup.github.io/relate/) [tsinfer documentation](https://tskit.dev/tsinfer/docs/latest/index.html) [ARG-Needle](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10181934/) Summary statistics remain essential for robustness, speed, visualization, and model checking.

- **Changed within the last 18 months:** SINGER was published online in September 2025. It samples genome-wide genealogies from a posterior rather than reporting only one inferred ARG, directly foregrounding uncertainty; its reported scale was hundreds, not biobank-scale hundreds of thousands, of genomes. [Deng, Nielsen & Song 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41588-025-02317-9) This strengthens the case for teaching ARG uncertainty, not just ARG outputs.

- In low- or medium-coverage sequencing, hard genotype calls discard uncertainty. Simulations showed that call-based allele counting biases the SFS—multisample calling can understate rare variants, whereas single-sample calling can overstate them—thereby biasing demographic and selection analyses. [Han et al. 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3935184/)

- Genotype-likelihood methods integrate base and mapping-quality evidence instead of pretending uncertain genotypes are observed. ANGSD was built for this setting and explicitly recommends likelihood-based downstream analyses for low/medium coverage. [Korneliussen, Albrechtsen & Nielsen 2014](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12859-014-0356-4) They are required whenever depth/error uncertainty is material relative to the signal: typical ancient DNA, low-pass resequencing, pooled/uneven data, or analyses dominated by rare variants.

- Polymorphism and divergence are two views of the same mutational process on different parts of a genealogy: polymorphism is mutation on recent, uncoalesced within-species branches; divergence is mutation on branches separating sampled lineages/species after their shared ancestry. The boundary is not a biological wall—ancestral polymorphism persists beyond speciation, and gene flow can cross it. [McDonald & Kreitman 1991](https://www.nature.com/articles/351652a0) This is precisely why substitution-only dN/dS and polymorphism-plus-divergence tests answer different questions.

## What my chapter plan is MISSING

1. **An explicit “model checking and identifiability” thread, placed immediately after neutrality.** Teach that neutral expectations are conditional on mutation model, sampling, structure, recombination, and ascertainment—and that different histories can yield the same SFS/LD/diversity pattern. This is what stops dN/dS, F_ST, and scan statistics becoming labels.

2. **Data generation and genotype uncertainty, before the SFS.** Low-coverage GLs, reference bias, missingness, mapping around paralogs/SVs, ancestral-state error, phasing, and ascertainment determine the observed SFS and LD. Put a compact “from reads to population-genetic data” section before any summary statistic.

3. **Population structure, migration, and sampling design, before or integrated with the coalescent/SFS.** Structure is not merely a chapter-12 concern: it alters Ne, SFS, LD, F_ST, gene trees, and the null for selection scans. A panmictic coalescent taught without an immediate structured counterexample will mislead.

4. **Make recombination/linked selection precede Ne as a genome-wide property.** Your order presently risks reifying one Ne. Introduce Ne as a process-specific, local and historical effective size after showing why selection and recombination make it vary along genomes.

5. **Add gene conversion/gBGC explicitly to the forces section.** It has disproportionate downstream value for codon models, substitution landscapes, convergence, and false adaptation claims.

6. **Add a short forward-time allele-frequency primer before backward coalescence.** One page on mutation–drift–selection–migration, fixation probability, and scaled parameters makes the backward model intuitive. Do not derive diffusion theory.

7. **Move divergence versus polymorphism slightly earlier, as a recurring bridge rather than the final section.** Introduce it once mutation, coalescence, and Ne are in place; return to it at the end through dN/dS, McDonald–Kreitman logic, and species divergence versus population split time.

What can be lighter: exhaustive derivations of Wright–Fisher/diffusion results; named neutrality tests; a tool survey beyond one GL example and one ARG example; and detailed ARG algorithms. For this reader, assumptions, failure modes, and counterfactual signatures are higher value.

## Caveats and open questions

- The neutralist/selectionist controversy is genuinely unresolved because authors differ over the proposition being tested: most substitutions, most polymorphisms, direct selection, linked selection, or adaptive phenotype change. The 2023 review explicitly argues that conflating these produces artificial disagreement. [review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37621151/)

- “Most of the genome is affected by linked selection” is strongly supported for the particular human analysis above, but its numerical 95% figure must not be generalized to other organisms with different recombination landscapes, mating systems, genome architectures, and Ne. [Pouyet et al.](https://elifesciences.org/articles/36317)

- SFS, LD, and ARG inferences are not automatically more causal because they are genome-wide; selection, structure, recombination-map error, mutation heterogeneity, and unmodeled sampling can still bias them. The newer SINGER work explicitly identifies uncertainty and model misspecification as unresolved ARG issues. [SINGER](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41588-025-02317-9)

- I verified a clear within-18-month change—SINGER’s September 2025 publication—but did not find a peer-reviewed source sufficient to declare that ARGs have displaced summary statistics by August 2026. The supported conclusion is narrower: ARG-aware inference is rapidly expanding while summary-statistic methods remain routine and necessary.
